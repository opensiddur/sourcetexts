# Concluding prayers: printed 753–790 / Internet Archive n777–n814

The 38 leaves cover marriage, meals and Grace After Meals, miscellaneous blessings,
bedtime Shema, children's bedtime prayer, and the prayer for the State of Israel.
The numbered page-range JSON files preserve the initial source-first readings.
`readings.json` contains the 146 reviewed bilingual text units; `notes.json` contains
42 printed note paragraphs, and `rubrics.json` the instructions. `corrections.json`
records changes made after inspecting enlarged scan bands. Corresponding per-page
readings, Hebrew text, and secondary comparison slices are in the adjacent directories.

`initial_comparison.json` and `comparison.json` are machine comparisons, not error
rates. Their default unresolved verdicts are retained: the correction ledger records
specific scan decisions, but does not adjudicate every punctuation, whitespace, or
encoding-convention difference. The final comparison has 28 consonants, 184 vowels, 95 whitespace difference records.

Substantive scan decisions include חשמוני without an alef, the printed spelling
כנורות, מזבחך with the printed pointing, and ופרוס and ולשמור in the Israel prayer.
Wikisource's extra Israel-grown produce alternatives in Me'ein Shalosh are not
attributed to Birnbaum. Hebrew food alternatives continue on printed 772. Birnbaum's
English uses a generic nourishment translation; no English alternatives were invented.
Psalm 91 includes verse 15 and the Hebrew repeat of verse 16 absent from the secondary
slice. Logical holam/shin combinations retain both Unicode marks. See the explicit
before/after ledger for the individual corrections, including stress and punctuation.

The final three English page numbers (786, 788, 790) are missing from both imported
number sources. They were read directly from scan pages 811, 813, 815 (IA leaves
n810, n812, n814). The importer records this evidence and regenerates pages.json;
the final paired English translation is body matter, not back matter.

Editorial encoding choices are separate from the printed text:

- The user selected wine last after the wedding meal; ceremony order remains wine first.
- The wedding, ordinary, and Brit Milah meals share the ordinary Grace text. Brit Milah's
  special Harachaman is inserted after Bamarom, as its printed instruction specifies.
- Calendar additions use the existing Hebrew calendar, including Israel/diaspora Yom Tov
  differences. Migdol depends on a day with Musaf, not on the service being compiled.
- Unknown meal context retains MAYBE: zimmun, guest/host and family wording, and food type.
- Biblical verses use milestones; existing differing witnesses retain biblical sources
  with local correspondences. Partial Deuteronomy quotations in the Israel prayer are
  bounded source spans. Shared text retains the additional printed-page evidence.

Verification: all 146 text units and 42 notes are found in the compiled parallel text
using normalized letters (not a claim of exact punctuation identity). Seven dated meal
scenarios resolve every conditional, including Shabbat Rosh Hodesh and 22 Nisan in
Israel versus the diaspora. The 40-page focused proof has hierarchical bookmarks.
