## Conditional and correspondence decisions

- The addressable Sabbath Musaf service is `urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/musaf`; its service declaration sets Musaf true and the other service flags false. Its nested headings form the bookmark hierarchy.
- Ordinary Sabbath and Sabbath–Rosh Hodesh central blessings are mutually exclusive, including both days of a two-day Rosh Hodesh. A festival or Chol Hamoed requires the later festival Musaf, not this Sabbath Amidah. This differs from Sabbath Shacharit on Chol Hamoed. The closing sequence is outside the Amidah gate, ready for that later return.
- The scan says “during leap year,” without a month cutoff. The atonement phrase uses the Hebrew leap-year feature for the whole year; with no year supplied, it remains MAYBE with its rubric. Only the added phrase is enclosed.
- Repetition passages require a minyan and repetition; Kaddishes require a minyan. Whole-section gates live in the transcluding files. Private recitation retains the silent Amidah and omits Kedushah, priestly blessing, and all Kaddishes.
- Psalm 92 and its Kaddish have a Sabbath gate. Psalm 27 and its Kaddish use the user's previously settled seasonal boundary: through 21 Tishrei in Israel and 22 Tishrei in the diaspora.
- Canonical prayer/poem/Talmud/Mishnah URNs identify unique texts. Existing prayers are transcluded at their existing correspondences; Numbers 28 and other full verses use biblical URNs. Repeated verses with different English wording retain a local correspondence plus biblical `source`. Repeated stanzas and Mishnah units use milestones; higher-level sections use divisions.

The incense was composed of balm, onycha, galbanum, and frankincense, seventy minas’ weight of each; myrrh, cassia, spikenard, and saffron, sixteen minas’ weight of each; twelve minas of costus; three minas of an aromatic bark; nine minas of cinnamon; nine kabs of Karsina lye; three seahs and three kabs of Cyprus wine—if Cyprus wine could not be obtained, strong white wine might be substituted for it—a fourth of a kab of Sodom salt, and a minute quantity of ma‘aleh ashan [a smoke-producing ingredient]. Rabbi Nathan says: A minute quantity of Jordan amber was also required. If one added honey to the mixture, he rendered the incense unfit for sacred use; and if he left out one of its required ingredients, he was subject to the penalty of death.

Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel says: The balm required for the incense is a resin exuding from the balsam trees. The Karsina lye was rubbed over the onycha to refine it; the Cyprus wine was used to steep the onycha in it so as to make it more pungent. Though mei raglayim might have been good for that purpose, it was not decent to bring it into the Temple.

## New commentary and source notes

Targets name the annotated prayer or stanza; the order below follows the importer. The page sequence above records their printed locations.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/musaf/amidah

the prayer added after Shaḥarith, corresponds to the additional sacrifices on Sabbaths and festive days over and above the regular daily tamid offered in the Temple.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:prayer:amidah/qedushah/naaritz

equals שיח סוד; compare שיח סוד שרפי קודש in the Sephardic Siddur. נעריצך כשיח סוד is based on אל נערץ בסוד קדושים (Psalm 89:8) where the meaning is: “God is revered in the council of the holy ones.”

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/musaf/amidah/kedushah/shema

and the concluding words of the Shema were inserted here in the fifth century, when special government officials were posted in the synagogues to prevent the congregational proclamation of God’s Oneness. Toward the end of the service, when the spies had left, the Shema was thus recited in an abridged form.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/musaf/kaddish

is said between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur; otherwise only לעילא is said. In some rituals לעילא is repeated throughout the year. לעילא לעילא is the Targum’s rendering of מעלה מעלה (Deuteronomy 28:43).

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/musaf/kaddish

(“consolations”), occurring in the Kaddish as a synonym of praise, probably refers to prophetic works such as the Book of Isaiah, called Books of Consolation, which contain hymns of praise as well as Messianic prophecies.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:poem:ein_kelohenu

forms the acrostic אמן, ברוך אתה. Each of the three letters of אמן is repeated four times, totalling twelve. Rashi, in his Siddur, points out that אין כאלהינו is recited on Sabbaths and festivals, when the Amidah prayer is limited to seven benedictions instead of the nineteen benedictions contained in the regular Shemoneh Esreh, in order to bring the blessings to a total of nineteen. En Kelohenu was composed during the period of the Geonim.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:mishnah:tamid/7/4/shabbat

“the great Sabbath,” a symbolic description of the world to come, a foretaste of which is offered by the weekly Sabbath.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:talmud:megillah/28b/tana_devei_eliyahu

a midrashic collection of mysterious authorship, consists of two parts: Seder Eliyyahu Rabba (thirty-one chapters) and Seder Eliyyahu Zuta (twenty-five chapters). According to the Talmud (Kethubboth 106a), Elijah frequently visited Rabbi Anan (third century) and taught him Seder Eliyyahu. This work, which has been named “the jewel of aggadic literature,” repeatedly emphasizes the importance of diligence in the study of the Torah.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:talmud:megillah/28b/tana_devei_eliyahu
