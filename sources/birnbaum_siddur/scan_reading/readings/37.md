# Printed page 37 (Hebrew; scan 62, leaf 61)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `37`. Opens mid-passage, finishing the Rosh Ḥodesh
musaf from page 35, and closes at the end of a mishnah.

## The page

- the tail of the Rosh Ḥodesh passage, `וְנִסְכֵּיהֶם, חֲצִי הַהִין`.
- `משנה זבחים, פרק ה` — **Eizehu Mekoman** begins, mishnayoth `א`, `ב`, `ג`.

The words are in `hebrew/37.txt`.

## The mishnayoth are numbered, and the numeral is part of the text

Each mishnah opens with a Hebrew letter and a full stop, set on the line with the words and
followed by a wider space: `א. אֵיזֶֽהוּ מְקוֹמָן`, `ב. פָּרִים הַנִּשְׂרָפִים`,
`ג. חַטֹּאת הַצִּבּוּר`. They run א through ז over this page and the next.

This is a numbered sequence rather than a heading apparatus: the numeral sits inline, not
above or beside, and the Wikisource edition carries it inside the span's own text rather
than in a `כותרת` span — so both witnesses have it and the comparison sees it as words.
Recorded in `hebrew/37.txt` for that reason, unlike a heading or a citation.

## A citation naming a chapter rather than verses

`משנה זבחים, פרק ה` — a work, a comma, then `פרק` and the chapter. Not the
`chapter, verse–verse` shape the scriptural citations take, because there are no verses to
give; the mishnah numbers are in the text itself.

The foot of the page is blank.

## The comparison

**Five differences over 185 words, and every one is qamats qatan.** No metegs, no dageshes,
no punctuation, nothing needing a crop — the cleanest page of the unit by a wide margin,
and the second in a row with nothing to adjudicate beyond the standing rule.

Worth naming why: this page is almost entirely Mishnah, and the two witnesses are copying
the same well-established text. The pages that generate differences are the ones where the
edition's editors had something to decide.
