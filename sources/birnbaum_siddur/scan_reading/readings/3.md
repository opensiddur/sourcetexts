# Printed page 3 (Hebrew; scan 28, leaf 27)

The first page of Birkhoth ha-Shaḥar. **No running head**, and the page number `3` is
printed, centred, at the foot, below the footnotes.

That is the book's rule and not an accident of the first page: a page that carries a
section heading gives the heading the top of the measure and puts its number at the foot,
and a page that does not carries a running head with the number beside it at the top.
Page 5 is the first page of this unit to show the other half of the rule. Page 1 was read
as though running heads began at page 3; they begin at page 5.

## Heading (Hebrew, centred)

    בִּרְכוֹת הַשַּֽׁחַר

The facing English page 4 heads the section PRELIMINARY MORNING SERVICE. **The two
headings are in different languages**, as Shaḥarith li-Yladim's are and unlike the
Amidah, where SHEMONEH ESREH stands in English on both sides of the opening. This is the
configuration that produced a heading typeset backwards once before, so it is worth
measuring rather than looking at.

## The page

Two sections, each with a centred heading, and rubrics set in **English roman** in the
Hebrew column — the same practice as pages 1 and 81.

Defective צִיצִת twice, not צִיצִית — the same spelling page 1 sets in the blessing over
the arba kanfoth. The footnote below the rule spells the catchword plene, ציצית: the
commentary and the liturgy are set from different copy, and the difference is his.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

**Two notes, and the second does not finish on this page.** Each is keyed by an
unvocalized Hebrew catchword set in bold. This is the first page of the conversion where
a note runs across the opening: the second breaks mid-sentence at the foot of this page
and finishes at the foot of the facing English page 4.

### Note 1

Catchwords אהליך, משכנותיך — two lemmata opening one note, not two notes.

    אהליך, משכנותיך are interpreted in the Talmud (Sanhedrin 105b) to refer to
    synagogues and schools. עת רצון is taken to mean the time of public worship
    (Berakhoth 8a).

A second lemma, עת רצון, is glossed inside the same note rather than being given a note of
its own. Both lemmata are in Mah Tovu, so the note annotates one passage.

### Note 2

Catchword ציצית.

    ציצית is a continual reminder of our obligation to keep God's commands. The
    purple-blue thread (פתיל תכלת) entwined in the tsitsith was originally its chief
    distinction. When, however, it became impossible to procure the special dye
    required, it was made permissible to use white threads alone. Why blue? "Because
    this color resembles the sea, the sea resembles the sky . . ." (Menaḥoth 43b).

    Four threads are taken, of which one (the shammash) is considerably longer than the
    rest, for each of the four corners of the tallith. The four threads are drawn
    through a small hole or eyelet and the ends brought together. A double knot is tied
    close to the margin of the tallith; the shammash is then

— and there the page ends, mid-sentence, mid-word in the compositor's own hyphenation of
nothing: the break falls after `then`. The continuation is at the foot of page 4.

`tsitsith`, `shammash` and `tallith` are italic; the Hebrew inside the note is not bold,
only the opening catchword is.

**The break is the 1949 compositor's measure and is not encoded.** A rendered PDF sets to
a different measure, so a break authored where this one falls would land in the wrong
place. The note is one note; where it broke is recorded here and nowhere else.
