## The page

Four rubrics, all English roman, each governing one passage:

    When placing the tefillin on the left arm:
    When placing the tefillin on the forehead:
    When winding the retsuah three times round the middle finger:

`tefillin` and `retsuah` are italic within their roman rubrics, as `tallith` is on pages 3
and 5.

In order: the tail of the meditation; the blessing over binding the tefillin; the blessing
over the commandment of tefillin; Barukh Shem; the passage beginning וּמֵחָכְמָתְךָ; and
the three verses from Hosea said while winding the strap.

**Barukh Shem is set as its own line here**, between the second blessing and the
וּמֵחָכְמָתְךָ passage. It is the same text the children's page prints at page 1, so it
carries the same URN and this unit transcludes rather than re-emits it.

Semicolons at הַנְּטוּיָה, שְׁמוֹ, תְּבִינֵֽנִי and וְקָמָי — checked deliberately, after
page 3 showed that a semicolon and a comma are one mark at band magnification.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

**One note, and it does not run across the opening** — the first self-contained note since
page 1. Keyed by the catchword ומחכמתך, it names the passage's source as a sixteenth
century kabbalistic commentary on the Siddur by Moses Albas of northwest Africa.

So the unit now shows all three arrangements: a note wholly on its page (1, 7), a note
that runs over to the facing English page (3, 5), and a page whose foot carries both a
continuation and a fresh apparatus (4).

## Two readings corrected before committing

Both caught by the diff, both settled against the reading on the image:

- `זְרוֹעַ` read where the print sets **זְרוֹעוֹ** — the word ends in a vav.
- `לְהָנִֽיחַ` read in the blessing where the print sets **לְהַנִּֽיחַ**, patach under the
  he and dagesh in the nun. This is the same correction page 5 needed, in the same word,
  so the spelling is his and consistent.

Note that the two occurrences on this page are **not** the same word: the blessing sets
לְהַנִּֽיחַ and the meditation sets לְהָנִיחַ. The transcription distinguishes them and the
print does too; flattening them would be a correction rather than a reading.
