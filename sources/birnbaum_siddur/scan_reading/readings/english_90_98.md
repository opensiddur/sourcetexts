# Printed pages 90, 92, 94, 96, 98 (English; facing 89, 91, 93, 95, 97)

These five were taken from the en.wikisource page-by-page transcription of THIS print —
a human transcription, page-scoped, of the same scan — and checked against the page
images. That is a different relationship from the Hebrew side, where the available
transcription is a reconstructed text rather than a witness to this book.

Verified on the image rather than taken on trust:

- **The three-column Ya'aleh v'Yavo block on 90** runs `Rosh Ḥodesh | Pesaḥ | Sukkoth`
  left to right, the MIRROR of Hebrew page 89's `Sukkoth | Pesaḥ | Rosh Ḥodesh`. Same
  reading order on both sides; only the physical placement flips. Same as pages 87/88.
- **The Hallel cross-reference gives a different page on each side** — Hebrew 97 says
  "Hallel (page 565)", English 98 says "Hallel (page 566)". Cropped and read at 3x on
  both. Each side points at its own language's Hallel. Not a transcription slip.
- **The Modim block on 92** is two columns split by a rule, as on 91, with the same
  heading over the left one.
- **Page 96 sets the Ten Days rubric with the asterisk** — "* Between Rosh Hashanah and
  Yom Kippur say:" — matching page 95's wording, "say" and not "substitute".

## What the English side has that the Hebrew does not

- The numbered scripture citations (¹ ² ³) keyed to superscripts in the running text.
- Bracketed editorial glosses inside the translation: "in Shushan the capital [of
  Persia]" (94). Birnbaum's own, and part of his English.

## What the Hebrew side has that the English does not

- The speaker labels in small roman capitals ("Reader") set inline before the words.
  The English sets "Reader:" as its own centred italic line instead, and at the Kedushah
  responses gives no label at all, translating לְעֻמָּתָם as "Those opposite them say".
