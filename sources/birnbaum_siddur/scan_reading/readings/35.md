## Birnbaum's count checks out

His footnote under page 33 says Ana B'khoaḥ "has six words to each of its seven lines" and
that the forty-two words stand for the forty-two-letter Name. Counted across the two pages,
the poem is exactly that: seven lines, six words each. The note is not a generality about
the poem — it describes *this* setting of it, and it is right.

That is worth more than a curiosity. It means the line division is verifiable, and an
encoding that lost or added a line would be contradicted by the book's own apparatus.

## Two conditional passages, and the rubrics are English on the Hebrew page

Two passages are said only on some days, each introduced by a **centred English rubric on
the Hebrew page** and then its own citation line:

    On Sabbath say:
    במדבר כח, ט–י
    וּבְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת שְׁנֵי כְבָשִׂים …

    On Rosh Ḥodesh say:
    במדבר כח, יא–טו
    וּבְרָאשֵׁי חׇדְשֵׁיכֶם תַּקְרִֽיבוּ …

Three things about this the encoding has to carry:

- **The rubric is English and it is on the Hebrew page.** Every rubric so far in this unit
  has been. `Rosh Ḥodesh` is set in italic within the roman rubric, and the ḥet carries an
  underdot — his transliteration, to be set as printed.
- **These are additions, not substitutions.** Nothing is negated or replaced; on an ordinary
  weekday neither passage is said and the page simply has less on it. That is two plain
  `j:conditional` blocks, not the asterisk-and-negation pair the Amidah's inserts need.
- **The citation line belongs inside the conditional**, between the rubric and the words. It
  is not a heading for the page.

This is also the first place in this unit where the day matters at all, which bears on the
unit's occasion: `chol/shacharit/…` with conditionals, as the Amidah has it, and not `all`.
