### [Kedushah]

Rubric:

    When the Reader repeats the Shemoneh Esreh, the following Kedushah is said:

NOTE: this rubric does not condition the passage on a day — it conditions it on the
Reader's repetition, which is a recitation and not an occasion.

    נְקַדֵּשׁ אֶת שִׁמְךָ בָּעוֹלָם כְּשֵׁם שֶׁמַּקְדִּישִׁים אוֹתוֹ בִּשְׁמֵי מָרוֹם,
    כַּכָּתוּב עַל יַד נְבִיאֶֽךָ: וְקָרָא זֶה אֶל זֶה וְאָמַר:

    קָדוֹשׁ, קָדוֹשׁ, קָדוֹשׁ יְיָ צְבָאוֹת; מְלֹא כָל הָאָֽרֶץ כְּבוֹדוֹ.

    [Reader]  לְעֻמָּתָם בָּרוּךְ יֹאמֵֽרוּ–

    בָּרוּךְ כְּבוֹד יְיָ מִמְּקוֹמוֹ.

    [Reader]  וּבְדִבְרֵי קָדְשְׁךָ כָּתוּב לֵאמֹר:

    יִמְלֹךְ יְיָ לְעוֹלָם, אֱלֹהַֽיִךְ צִיּוֹן לְדֹר וָדֹר; הַלְלוּיָהּ.

"Reader" is set in small roman capitals INLINE, to the LEFT of the Hebrew it labels,
on the same line. Left is the END of an RTL line, not its beginning, so do not read the
placement as "before": it is a marginal speaker label sitting beside the line, not a word
in the reading stream. What it labels is the line it shares, which is the Reader's lead-in
-- לעמתם ברוך יאמרו and ובדברי קדשך כתוב לאמר. The three responses (קדוש, ברוך כבוד, ימלוך)
carry no label, being the congregation's.
The em-dash after יֹאמֵֽרוּ is printed and belongs to the text: the Reader's line runs
into the response.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom, below a rule) — Birnbaum's commentary, by Hebrew lemma

    זכרנו and the other special prayers, added between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur,
    are not mentioned in the Talmud. They were inserted during the geonic period.

    גבורות, the second benediction recounting the omnipotence of God, includes phrases
    from Psalms 145:14; 146:7; Daniel 12:2; I Samuel 2:6.
