# Printed page 33 (Hebrew; scan 58, leaf 57)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `33`. Opens mid-sentence, finishing Bar Kappara from
page 31, and closes mid-poem.

- the tail of Pittum ha-Ketoreth, ending `לֹא תַקְטִֽירוּ מִמֶּֽנּוּ אִשֶּׁה לַייָ`.
- three verses, **each on its own centred line**: `יְיָ צְבָאוֹת עִמָּֽנוּ`,
  `יְיָ צְבָאוֹת, אַשְׁרֵי אָדָם`, `יְיָ, הוֹשִֽׁיעָה`.
- `אַתָּה סֵֽתֶר לִי`, one centred line.
- `וְעָרְבָה לַייָ`, one paragraph.
- `מסכת יומא לג, א` — Abaye's order of the sacrifices, `אַבַּיֵּי הֲוָה מְסַדֵּר`.
- **Ana B'khoaḥ**, two of its seven lines, each centred, the page turn cutting the poem.

The words are in `hebrew/33.txt`.

## The poem's shape is stated by the note beneath it

Birnbaum's own footnote says Ana B'khoaḥ "has six words to each of its seven lines" and
that the forty-two words stand for the forty-two-letter Name. So the line division is not a
typographic choice to be reproduced approximately — **the count is the point**, and an
encoding that reflowed the lines would contradict the note printed under them. Seven lines
of six words, as page 35 will finish it.

This is the same relationship printed page 11's Yigdal had: his footnote tells you what the
lines are for. Where he says so, believe the note over the measure.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

**Two notes in one block**, each opening with its own unpointed Hebrew lemma and a paragraph
indent, neither running across the opening:

1. `שלמים` — "is here interpreted to imply completion (from שלם, 'to be finished')."
2. `אנא בכח` — "is a rhymed prayer. It has six words to each of its seven lines. According
   to the Kabbalists, the forty-two words of this poem represent the name of God which is
   composed of forty-two letters. Though it has been credited to the *Tanna* Neḥunya ben
   ha-Kanah (first century) its author is unknown."

Two notes under one rule, run on as consecutive indented paragraphs, is how a page with more
than one commentary note is set — the same shape page 24 uses for the tail of one note
followed by another.

## The comparison

Ten differences over 159 words, all settled for the print.

**Five are qamats qatan**, settled by rule. **One is a meteg** the edition drops,
`הַמֶּֽלֶךְ`, checked at 12×.

**Four are one word, four times**: the print sets `לַהֲטָבַת` / `וַהֲטָבַת` and the edition
sets a dagesh in the teth that is not there. At 25× the teth's interior is plainly open, as
is the beth's beside it. Four occurrences pointed the same way in the print and the same way
in the edition — the two witnesses simply disagree, consistently, about one word.
