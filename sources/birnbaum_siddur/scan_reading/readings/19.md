# Printed page 19 (Hebrew; scan 44, leaf 43)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `19` at the top outer corner. Opens mid-sentence,
finishing the Yehi Ratzon from page 17, and closes mid-verse in Genesis 22.

## A speaker label, the first in this unit

Against the second line, set in the **margin** in small roman capitals:

    Reader

The Amidah unit records speaker labels as "Hebrew-side only, small roman capitals set
inline". This one is not inline — it stands in the left margin beside the line it governs,
the way a stage direction does. Same vocabulary, different placement, and the placement is
the print's rather than the encoding's: it is an instruction naming who says what follows,
and takes an `instruction:role/reader` URN as the Amidah's do.

Check page 20 before concluding anything about how the English side sets it. The Amidah's
English page gives "Reader:" as its own centred italic line rather than a marginal label.

## The comparison, and a slice error worth naming

Nine differences: seven qamats qatan settled by rule, a holam and two metegs — plus one
correction to the reading. The reading had a paragraph break after
`לְהִשְׁתַּעְבֶּד־לָךְ.`, where the print runs straight on; what falls there is the
marginal `Reader` label and not a new paragraph, and marking the label with a break put a
division in `hebrew/19.txt` that the page does not have.

**The first run reported twenty-two consonantal differences, and every one was an
artifact.** The passage beginning `וּתְנֵֽנוּ הַיּוֹם` has no section of its own in the
Wikisource foundation page; it lives inside a variant section named `ב`, which the slice
had skipped. Twenty-two words missing from one side, twenty-two consonantal differences
out the other.

That is now the fourth time slice-building has manufactured differences rather than found
them, and **the signature is the same every time: a word-count gap on one side, and a wall
of consonantal differences.** A real disagreement about consonants is rare in this book —
zero in two thousand words once the slices are right. So a page reporting consonantal
differences by the dozen is reporting a boundary problem, and the fix is to find the
missing or duplicated section before anyone looks at a crop.
