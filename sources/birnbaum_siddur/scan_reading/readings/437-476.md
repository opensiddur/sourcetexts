## Encoding decisions

- The service is addressable as urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah and supplies a bookmark hierarchy. The Torah service, Amidah, post-Amidah passages, and winter Psalm collection have separate addresses.
- Half Kaddish after Torah, Tzidkatekha, and full Kaddish are outside the Amidah. The future festival Amidah can return to the common conclusion. Shabbat Chol Hamoed uses the Shabbat Amidah; Yom Tov uses the later festival Amidah referenced in the print.
- Reused text comes from the previously scan-read Ashrei, Uva Letzion, weekday Torah service, weekday Amidah, Shabbat Retzeh paragraph, Kaddish, Alenu and Al Tira. The weekday Torah call is limited to its opening and Barukh Shenatan: the following verses and weekday supplications do not occur here.
- The new Atah Eḥad paragraph has a prayer URN. Complete Psalms have biblical chapter and verse URNs. Verses already realized in this edition retain local occurrence milestones and biblical source pointers. The Tzidkatekha compilation has a section URN and biblical verse correspondences.
- Preserve the local אדני in ברוך אדני יום יום in Uva Letzion (441), rather than the earlier יי. Preserve Psalm 134’s English “Come bless” and “Lift up your hands”, differing from its weekday-Arvit printing.
- Seasonal and occasion gates belong in the including service, leaving the prayers and Psalms independently reusable. Only the second לעילא is conditional, using the existing standardized Kaddish instruction. Printed parentheses are not added around already conditional text.
- Logical holam and shin/sin dots are separate; qamats uses U+05B8. Secondary-only rubrics and textual emendations are not imported. Psalm 127 retains מאחרי שבת without an added maqaf; Psalm 128 retains כשתילי with its printed yod; Psalm 104 retains תוסף with vav and הללויה as one word.

- Additional Torah variants: “The Lord is pleased, for the sake of his righteousness” (448), “clothed in righteousness” and “good instruction; forsake” (450). Surrounding verses remain shared.
- Winter Psalm boundaries confirmed by the user: after Simchat Torah (23 Tishrei in Israel, 24 in the Diaspora) through 14 Nisan.

### Atah Eḥad — 453/454

אַתָּה אֶחָד וְשִׁמְךָ אֶחָד, וּמִי כְעַמְּךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל גּוֹי אֶחָד בָּאָרֶץ. תִּפְאֶרֶת גְּדֻלָּה, וַעֲטֶרֶת יְשׁוּעָה, יוֹם מְנוּחָה וּקְדֻשָּׁה לְעַמְּךָ נָתָתָּ. אַבְרָהָם יָגֵל, יִצְחָק יְרַנֵּן, יַעֲקֹב וּבָנָיו יָנוּחוּ בוֹ. מְנוּחַת אַהֲבָה וּנְדָבָה, מְנוּחַת אֱמֶת וֶאֱמוּנָה, מְנוּחַת שָׁלוֹם וְשַׁלְוָה וְהַשְׁקֵט וָבֶטַח, מְנוּחָה שְׁלֵמָה שָׁאַתָּה רוֹצֶה בָּהּ. יַכִּירוּ בָנֶיךָ וְיֵדְעוּ כִּי מֵאִתְּךָ הִיא מְנוּחָתָם, וְעַל מְנוּחָתָם יַקְדִּישׁוּ אֶת שְׁמֶךָ.
Thou art One and thy name is One; and who is like thy people Israel unique on earth? Thou gavest thy people a crown of distinction, a crown of triumph—a day of rest and holiness. Abraham and Isaac rejoiced on the Sabbath; Jacob and his children found rest in it. It is a rest granted in gracious love, a true and genuine rest, a rest that yields peace and tranquility, serenity and confidence, a perfect rest with which thou art pleased. May thy children realize and know that their rest comes from thee, and by keeping the Sabbath they hallow thy name.

שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת לְדָוִד. יְיָ, לֹא גָבַהּ לִבִּי, וְלֹא רָמוּ עֵינַי, וְלֹא הִלַּכְתִּי בִּגְדֹלוֹת וּבְנִפְלָאוֹת מִמֶּנִּי.

A Pilgrim Song by David. O Lord, my heart is not haughty, nor are my eyes lofty; neither do I concern myself with matters too great and too wonderful for me.

### Verse 2

אִם לֹא שִׁוִּיתִי וְדוֹמַמְתִּי, נַפְשִׁי כְּגָמֻל עֲלֵי אִמּוֹ, כַּגָּמֻל עָלַי נַפְשִׁי.

Surely I have soothed and stilled my soul, like a weaned child with its mother; my soul is with me like a weaned child.

### Verse 3

יַחֵל יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶל יְיָ, מֵעַתָּה וְעַד עוֹלָם.

O Israel, put your hope in the Lord, henceforth and forever.

## Psalm 132 — printed 475–476

### Verse 1

שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת. זְכוֹר יְיָ לְדָוִד אֵת כָּל עֻנּוֹתוֹ.

A Pilgrim Song. O Lord, remember David and all his affliction;

### Verse 2

אֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּע לַיְיָ, נָדַר לַאֲבִיר יַעֲקֹב.

how he swore to the Lord, and vowed to the Mighty One of Jacob:

### Verse 3

אִם אָבֹא בְּאֹהֶל בֵּיתִי, אִם אֶעֱלֶה עַל עֶרֶשׂ יְצוּעָי.

“I will not enter my house, I will not lie on my bed,

### Verse 4

אִם אֶתֵּן שְׁנַת לְעֵינָי, לְעַפְעַפַּי תְּנוּמָה.

I will not give sleep to my eyes, nor slumber to my eyelids,

### Verse 5

עַד אֶמְצָא מָקוֹם לַיְיָ, מִשְׁכָּנוֹת לַאֲבִיר יַעֲקֹב.

until I find a place for the Lord, a residence for the Mighty One of Jacob.”

### Verse 6

הִנֵּה שְׁמַעֲנוּהָ בְאֶפְרָתָה, מְצָאנוּהָ בִּשְׂדֵי יָעַר.

We heard of the ark in Ephrath; we found it in the fields of Yaar.

### Verse 7

נָבוֹאָה לְמִשְׁכְּנוֹתָיו, נִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה לַהֲדֹם רַגְלָיו.

Let us enter his dwelling; let us worship at his footstool.

### Verse 8

קוּמָה יְיָ לִמְנוּחָתֶךָ, אַתָּה וַאֲרוֹן עֻזֶּךָ.

Arise, O Lord, to thy resting-place, thou and thy glorious ark.

### Verse 9

כֹּהֲנֶיךָ יִלְבְּשׁוּ צֶדֶק, וַחֲסִידֶיךָ יְרַנֵּנוּ.

May thy priests be clothed in righteousness, and let thy faithful followers sing for joy.

### Verse 10

בַּעֲבוּר דָּוִד עַבְדֶּךָ, אַל תָּשֵׁב פְּנֵי מְשִׁיחֶךָ.

For the sake of thy servant David, reject not thy own anointed.

### Verse 11

נִשְׁבַּע יְיָ לְדָוִד, אֱמֶת לֹא יָשׁוּב מִמֶּנָּה; מִפְּרִי בִטְנְךָ אָשִׁית לְכִסֵּא לָךְ.

The Lord swore an oath to David, from which he will not swerve: “I will set one of your offspring on your throne.

### Verse 12

אִם יִשְׁמְרוּ בָנֶיךָ בְּרִיתִי, וְעֵדֹתִי זוֹ אֲלַמְּדֵם, גַּם בְּנֵיהֶם עֲדֵי עַד יֵשְׁבוּ לְכִסֵּא לָךְ.

If your children will keep my covenant and the laws which I teach them, their children also shall sit on your throne forever.”

### Verse 13

כִּי בָחַר יְיָ בְּצִיּוֹן, אִוָּהּ לְמוֹשָׁב לוֹ.

For the Lord has chosen Zion; he has desired it for his habitation.

### Verse 14

זֹאת מְנוּחָתִי עֲדֵי עַד; פֹּה אֵשֵׁב, כִּי אִוִּתִיהָ.

“This is my resting-place forever; here will I dwell, for I have desired it.

### Verse 15

צֵידָהּ בָּרֵךְ אֲבָרֵךְ, אֶבְיוֹנֶיהָ אַשְׂבִּיעַ לָחֶם.

I will richly bless its food supply; its needy I will satisfy with bread.

### Verse 16

וְכֹהֲנֶיהָ אַלְבִּישׁ יֶשַׁע, וַחֲסִידֶיהָ רַנֵּן יְרַנֵּנוּ.

Its priests will I clothe with triumph, and its godly shall sing for joy.

### Verse 17

שָׁם אַצְמִיחַ קֶרֶן לְדָוִד, עָרַכְתִּי נֵר לִמְשִׁיחִי.

There will I make David’s dynasty flourish; there have I prepared a lamp for my anointed.

### Verse 18

אוֹיְבָיו אַלְבִּישׁ בֹּשֶׁת, וְעָלָיו יָצִיץ נִזְרוֹ.

His foes I will clothe with shame, but his own crown shall shine.”

## Psalm 133 — printed 475–476

### Verse 1

שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת לְדָוִד. הִנֵּה מַה טּוֹב וּמַה נָּעִים שֶׁבֶת אַחִים גַּם יָחַד.

A Pilgrim Song by David. Behold, how good and pleasant it is for brethren to dwell in unity!

### Verse 2

כַּשֶּׁמֶן הַטּוֹב עַל הָרֹאשׁ, יֹרֵד עַל הַזָּקָן, זְקַן אַהֲרֹן, שֶׁיֹּרֵד עַל פִּי מִדּוֹתָיו.

It is like the precious oil upon the head, flowing down the beard, Aaron’s beard, that comes down upon the edge of his robes.

### Verse 3

כְּטַל חֶרְמוֹן שֶׁיֹּרֵד עַל הַרְרֵי צִיּוֹן; כִּי שָׁם צִוָּה יְיָ אֶת הַבְּרָכָה, חַיִּים עַד הָעוֹלָם.

It is like the dew of Mount Hermon, that comes down upon the hills of Zion; for there the Lord commanded the blessing, life for evermore.

## Psalm 134 — printed 475–476

### Verse 1

שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת. הִנֵּה בָּרְכוּ אֶת יְיָ, כָּל עַבְדֵי יְיָ, הָעֹמְדִים בְּבֵית יְיָ בַּלֵּילוֹת.

A Pilgrim Song. Come bless the Lord, all you servants of the Lord, who nightly stand in the house of the Lord.

### Verse 2

שְׂאוּ יְדֵכֶם קֹדֶשׁ וּבָרְכוּ אֶת יְיָ.

Lift up your hands in holiness, and bless the Lord.

### Verse 3

יְבָרֶכְךָ יְיָ מִצִּיּוֹן, עֹשֵׂה שָׁמַיִם וָאָרֶץ.

May the Lord, who made heaven and earth, bless you from Zion.

## Commentary

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/uva_letzion

ובא לציון

is postponed till Minḥah on Sabbaths and festivals. The reason is explained on page 131.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/opening_kaddish

לעילא לעילא

is said between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:prayer:amidah/atah_echad

אתה אחד ... בארץ

is based on Zechariah 14:9; I Chronicles 17:21. The <tei:hi rend="italic">Siddur</tei:hi> of Amram Gaon records the following variant in use during the ninth century: <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">הנח לנו ה׳ אלהינו כי אתה אבינו... ואל תהי צרה ויגון ביום מנוחתנו, מנוחת אהבה ונדבה, מנוחת אמת ואמונה</tei:foreign>.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:prayer:amidah/atah_echad

יעקב ובניו ינוחו

According to midrashic literature the patriarchs observed the Sabbath and fulfilled all the commands that were revealed later. The children of Abraham and of Isaac are not mentioned because Ishmael and Esau are not credited with the observance of the Sabbath.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/amidah/modim

מודים

is based on Psalms 79:13 and 55:18, namely: <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">נודה לך לעולם, לדור ודור נספר תהלתך</tei:foreign> and <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">ערב ובקר וצהרים אשיחה</tei:foreign>.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/amidah/modim_derabbanan

מודים דרבנן

is a composite of variants suggested by several rabbis of the Talmud (Sotah 40a).

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/amidah/hanukkah

מתתיהו

the leader of the Maccabean revolt against the Syrians, was the father of Simon who became High Priest in 141 before the common era. Hence, it is suggested that the epithet <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">כהן גדול</tei:foreign> refers to that fact. According to Sofrim 20:8, <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">מתתיהו</tei:foreign> and <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">חשמונאי</tei:foreign> were two different persons. There the reading is: <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">בימי מתתיהו... וחשמונאי ובניו</tei:foreign>.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/tzidkatkha

צדקתך צדק

is regarded as a form of <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">צדוק הדין</tei:foreign> said on the occasion of a death. According to tradition, Moses died on Sabbath afternoon. These three verses, containing the words <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">ה׳ אלהים אמת</tei:foreign>, are arranged in a reverse order in the Sephardic <tei:hi rend="italic">Siddur</tei:hi>. <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">צדקתך צדק</tei:foreign> is presumably a substitute for the <tei:hi rend="italic">Taḥanun</tei:hi> of the <tei:hi rend="italic">Minḥah</tei:hi> for weekdays.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/104

ברכי נפשי

(Psalm 104) is closely similar to the story of creation in Genesis. The psalmist celebrates God’s glory as seen in the forces of nature. It has been declared that it is worthwhile studying the Hebrew language for ten years in order to read Psalm 104 in the original.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/120

שיר המעלות

the title prefixed to the following fifteen psalms, is now generally understood to mean a psalm sung by the pilgrims as they went up to Jerusalem to celebrate the three pilgrim festivals in the center of national and religious life.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/120

Psalm 120 is directed against slanderers. They are doomed to severe punishment. They shall be pierced with sharp arrows and burned with the hot charcoal of the broom bush. <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">משך</tei:foreign> and <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">קדר</tei:foreign>, wild tribes, symbolize barbarian enemies.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/121

Psalm 121 is a perfect expression of trust in God, and has been on the lips of countless people when they felt the need of help beyond that which mortals can offer.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/122

Psalm 122 is a pilgrim’s recollection of a visit to Jerusalem and the many sacred memories associated with that magnificent city.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/123

Psalm 123 begins in the singular and continues in the plural. It is a hymn of faith composed in a time of distress, contemptuous scorn and mockery.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/124

Psalm 124 commemorates an escape from some imminent danger.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/125

Psalm 125 expresses the unshakable confidence of Israel in God, and the assurance that the evildoers shall perish.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/126

Psalm 126 is a song of those who have been redeemed from exile, and a hopeful prayer for those who have not yet returned. <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">כאפיקים בנגב</tei:foreign> like the hill streams of the Negev, dry in summer but becoming suddenly swollen torrents in the rains of the autumn.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/127

Psalm 127 is a warning against over-anxiety in any work. Man’s labor is in vain without God’s help. A numerous family is one of God’s special blessings; it secures for the parents influence and respect.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/128

Psalm 128 contains a picture of an ideal homelife. The welfare of the state depends upon virtuous family life.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/129

Psalm 129 is a song of deliverance and the overthrow of the wicked. On the flat roofs of oriental houses grass often springs up in the rainy season but quickly withers, yielding nothing useful. So the enemies of Zion shall be destroyed before their malicious schemes can mature.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/130

Psalm 130 is an expression of remorse for sin and a plea for forgiveness. Since God reveals himself as a forgiving God, Israel can hope and trust.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/131

Psalm 131 is a song of child-like humility. As the child that has gone through the troublesome process of weaning can lie happily in its mother’s arms, so the psalmist’s soul has found contentment and happiness through the discipline of humility.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/132

Psalm 132 contains the prayer that David’s efforts in establishing a sanctuary in Jerusalem should be well remembered and rewarded by God.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/133

Psalm 133 describes the blessing of unity and brotherly love. <tei:foreign xml:lang="he">כשמן הטוב</tei:foreign> as the fragrant oil with which Aaron was anointed diffused its fragrance all around, so the spirit of amity and mutual friendship is spread throughout the environment.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/minchah/psalms/134

Psalm 134 is a night-salutation addressed to the priests and Levites in the Temple, and their reply.
