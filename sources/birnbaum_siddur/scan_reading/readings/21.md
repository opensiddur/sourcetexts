## The comparison, and the nine words that were missing from both sides

**Read this section before trusting the one that follows it.** This page was first recorded
as the cleanest of the unit: 218 words, the slice matching at 218 exactly, zero whitespace
and zero consonantal differences. All of that was true and none of it meant anything,
because the page's **last line was absent from the reading and from the slice alike**:

    רִבּוֹנוֹ שֶׁל עוֹלָם, יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלְּפָנֶֽיךָ, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ וֵאלֹהֵי

Nine words, the opening of the Ribbono shel Olam that page 23 continues mid-sentence. The
comparison reports disagreements between its two sides; where both sides are missing the
same thing it reports nothing at all, and the exact agreement on the word count read as
confirmation rather than as the coincidence it was.

It surfaced when the slice was rebuilt mechanically from the printed page's own
transclusions and came back nine words longer. The reading now has them, verified on the
image: `וֵאלֹהֵי` with a tsere under the vav, where the edition sets a shva.

227 words. **Zero whitespace differences and zero consonantal differences.** Five vowel
differences.

One is a qamats qatan, settled by rule; one is the `וֵאלֹהֵי` above. The other four went
through `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`: four metegs, two of which the reading had and two of which the
transcription had.

That the metegs fall in both directions on one page is a small thing worth recording. It
means neither witness is systematically heavier with them here, so a page cannot be
settled by preferring one side's habit; each one still has to be looked at.
