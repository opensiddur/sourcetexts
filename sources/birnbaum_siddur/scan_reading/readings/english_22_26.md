# Printed pages 22, 24 and 26 (English; scan 47, 49, 51)

Three English pages facing Hebrew 21, 23 and 25. All three are transcribed on English
Wikisource at quality 4 from **this same scan**, so the transcription is checked against
the image and is not independent evidence; what is listed below is what was verified on
the image, not what was taken on trust.

Running head on each: the page number at the outer corner, `Preliminary Morning Service`
centred in italic.

## Contents

- **22** — the Akedah from `come back to you.` to `Abraham dwelt in Beersheba.`, then the
  opening of the Ribbono shel Olam, `Master of the world! May it be thy will, Lord our God
  and`, which the page turn cuts.
- **24** — the rest of that prayer; `Man should ever be God-fearing`; `Master of all
  worlds!`; and `However, we are thy people`, which closes at the end of a paragraph.
- **26** — `Therefore, it is our duty to give thanks`; the Shema verse; `Blessed be the
  name of his glorious majesty`; `Thou wast the same before the world was created`; and
  `Thou, Lord our God, art in heaven`.

## What the transcription gets wrong

Two, both on pages marked proofread at quality 4, and both settled at 7×:

1. **Page 24: `what thou hast, promised us`.** The print has no comma — `what thou hast
   promised us through Moses thy servant`. The comma is the transcriber's.
2. **Page 26: `deal Kindly with us`.** The print has a lower-case *k*. The capital is the
   transcriber's.

Everything else on the three pages matches, including the em dash in page 22's
`be blessed—because you have obeyed my voice`.

## The Shema is not centred on the English side

Page 26 sets `Hear, O Israel` and `Blessed be the name of his glorious majesty` as
**ordinary indented paragraphs**, at the same indent as the body paragraphs around them.
The facing Hebrew page centres both. The transcription wraps them in `{{c|…}}`, which is
its own reading of the layout and not this page's.

This matters for alignment rather than for prettiness: the two verses need URNs of their
own on both sides so they join, and the parting is there on both sides — but the English
side's parting is a paragraph break and not a centred display.

## No speaker labels on the English side

Hebrew page 25 carries `Reader` twice, both inline. **English page 26 carries neither.**
The Amidah's English pages set `Reader:` as their own centred italic line, so this is not
a general rule of the book but a fact about this opening, and every English page has to be
looked at rather than inferred from the Amidah.

## The footnote apparatus, and how it is set

These three pages settle the shape of the apparatus, which every unit so far has recorded
in prose and none has encoded.

**Two blocks, each under its own rule, in a fixed order.** Page 24 has both, and they are
separated:

    ────────────────────────────
    were posted in the synagogues to watch the services. … The additional
    word ובגלוי is not found in early texts.
        רבון כל העולמים is mentioned in Yoma 87b as a Yom Kippur prayer.
    ──────────
    ¹ Leviticus 26:42.

The first rule runs the width of the measure and separates the body from the **lemma-keyed
commentary**; the second is short and separates the commentary from the **numbered
scripture citations**. Commentary first, citations second.

**A note that runs across the opening is set once, not twice.** The block on page 24 opens
`were posted in the synagogues` — mid-sentence, with no lemma and no mark. It is the
continuation of the note begun under Hebrew page 23, whose lemma is `לעולם יהא`. The two
halves are one note, set continuously across the two feet of the opening.

**A commentary note carries no mark in the text.** `Man should ever be God-fearing` and
`Master of all worlds!` each have a note and neither carries a numeral or an asterisk; the
note finds its text through the quoted Hebrew lemma alone. Only the scripture citations are
numbered, and their numerals restart at 1 on each page.

**A lemma is unpointed, and a lemma covering a span joins its ends with an en dash.** The
note under Hebrew page 25 is keyed `קדש את שמך–ברבים`; page 24's are keyed `לעולם יהא` and
`רבון כל העולמים`. Hebrew also appears *inside* the note's prose, inline and unpointed —
`(בסתר)`, `ובגלוי`.

**The two apparatuses sit on different pages of the opening.** Page 22 has no apparatus at
all: the `בהר ה' יראה` note belongs to its text and is set under Hebrew page 21. Page 26
carries only `¹ Zephaniah 3:20.`; the `קדש את שמך` note for its text is under Hebrew page
25. So a note's page is decided by the apparatus it belongs to and not by the page its
text is on, and the English Wikisource transcription — which attaches every `<ref>` to the
English page — records the wrong one for the commentary notes.
