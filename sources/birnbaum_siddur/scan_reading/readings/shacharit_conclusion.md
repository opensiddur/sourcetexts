## Ashrei — Hebrew 127–129; English 128–130

אַשְׁרֵי יוֹשְׁבֵי בֵיתֶֽךָ; עוֹד יְהַלְלֽוּךָ סֶּֽלָה.

Happy are those who dwell in thy house; they are ever praising thee.

אַשְׁרֵי הָעָם שֶׁכָּֽכָה לּוֹ; אַשְׁרֵי הָעָם שֶׁיְיָ אֱלֹהָיו.

Happy the people that is so situated; happy the people whose God is the Lord.

תְּהִלָּה לְדָוִד{lb}אֲרוֹמִמְךָ, אֱלֹהַי הַמֶּֽלֶךְ, וַאֲבָרְכָה שִׁמְךָ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד.

A hymn of praise by David.{lb}I extol thee, my God the King,{lb}And bless thy name forever and ever.

{pb:129}בְּכָל יוֹם אֲבָרְכֶֽךָּ, וַאֲהַלְלָה שִׁמְךָ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד.

{pb:130}Every day I bless thee,{lb}And praise thy name forever and ever.

גָּדוֹל יְיָ וּמְהֻלָּל מְאֹד, וְלִגְדֻלָּתוֹ אֵין חֵֽקֶר.

Great is the Lord and most worthy of praise;{lb}His greatness is unsearchable.

דּוֹר לְדוֹר יְשַׁבַּח מַעֲשֶֽׂיךָ, וּגְבוּרֹתֶֽיךָ יַגִּֽידוּ.

One generation to another praises thy works;{lb}They recount thy mighty acts.

הֲדַר כְּבוֹד הוֹדֶֽךָ, וְדִבְרֵי נִפְלְאֹתֶֽיךָ אָשִֽׂיחָה.

On the splendor of thy glorious majesty{lb}And on thy wondrous deeds I meditate.

וֶעֱזוּז נוֹרְאֹתֶֽיךָ יֹאמֵֽרוּ, וּגְדֻלָּתְךָ אֲסַפְּרֶֽנָּה.

They speak of thy awe-inspiring might,{lb}And I tell of thy greatness.

זֵֽכֶר רַב טוּבְךָ יַבִּֽיעוּ, וְצִדְקָתְךָ יְרַנֵּֽנוּ.

They spread the fame of thy great goodness,{lb}And sing of thy righteousness.

חַנּוּן וְרַחוּם יְיָ, אֶֽרֶךְ אַפַּֽיִם וּגְדָל־חָֽסֶד.

Gracious and merciful is the Lord,{lb}Slow to anger and of great kindness.

טוֹב יְיָ לַכֹּל, וְרַחֲמָיו עַל כָּל מַעֲשָֽׂיו.

The Lord is good to all,{lb}And his mercy is over all his works.

יוֹדֽוּךָ יְיָ כָּל מַעֲשֶֽׂיךָ, וַחֲסִידֶֽיךָ יְבָרְכֽוּכָה.

All thy works praise thee, O Lord,{lb}And thy faithful followers bless thee.

כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתְךָ יֹאמֵֽרוּ, וּגְבוּרָתְךָ יְדַבֵּֽרוּ.

They speak of thy glorious kingdom,{lb}And talk of thy might,

לְהוֹדִֽיעַ לִבְנֵי הָאָדָם גְּבוּרֹתָיו, וּכְבוֹד הֲדַר מַלְכוּתוֹ.

To let men know thy mighty deeds,{lb}And the glorious splendor of thy kingdom.

מַלְכוּתְךָ מַלְכוּת כָּל עֹלָמִים, וּמֶמְשַׁלְתְּךָ בְּכָל דּוֹר וָדֹר.

Thy kingdom is a kingdom of all ages,{lb}And thy dominion is for all generations.

סוֹמֵךְ יְיָ לְכָל הַנֹּפְלִים, וְזוֹקֵף לְכָל הַכְּפוּפִים.

The Lord upholds all who fall,{lb}And raises all who are bowed down.

עֵינֵי כֹל אֵלֶֽיךָ יְשַׂבֵּרוּ, וְאַתָּה נוֹתֵן לָהֶם אֶת אָכְלָם בְּעִתּוֹ.

The eyes of all look hopefully to thee,{lb}And thou givest them their food in due season.

פּוֹתֵֽחַ אֶת יָדֶֽךָ, וּמַשְׂבִּֽיעַ לְכָל חַי רָצוֹן.

Thou openest thy hand,{lb}And satisfiest every living thing with favor.

צַדִּיק יְיָ בְּכָל דְּרָכָיו, וְחָסִיד בְּכָל מַעֲשָׂיו.

The Lord is righteous in all his ways,{lb}And gracious in all his deeds.

קָרוֹב יְיָ לְכָל קֹרְאָיו, לְכֹל אֲשֶׁר יִקְרָאֻֽהוּ בֶאֱמֶת.

The Lord is near to all who call upon him,{lb}To all who call upon him sincerely.

רְצוֹן יְרֵאָיו יַעֲשֶׂה, וְאֶת שַׁוְעָתָם יִשְׁמַע וְיוֹשִׁיעֵם.

He fulfills the desire of those who revere him;{lb}He hears their cry and saves them.

שׁוֹמֵר יְיָ אֶת כָּל אֹהֲבָיו, וְאֵת כָּל הָרְשָׁעִים יַשְׁמִיד.

The Lord preserves all who love him,{lb}But all the wicked he destroys.

תְּהִלַּת יְיָ יְדַבֶּר־פִּי; וִיבָרֵךְ כָּל בָּשָׂר שֵׁם קָדְשׁוֹ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד.

My mouth speaks the praise of the Lord;{lb}Let all creatures bless his holy name forever and ever.

{reader}וַאֲנַֽחְנוּ נְבָרֵךְ יָהּ מֵעַתָּה וְעַד עוֹלָם; הַלְלוּיָהּ.

We will bless the Lord henceforth and forever.{lb}Praise the Lord!

### psalm_20 — Hebrew 131–131; English 132–132

לַמְנַצֵּחַ, מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד.

For the Choirmaster; a psalm of David.

יַעַנְךָ יְיָ בְּיוֹם צָרָה; יְשַׂגֶּבְךָ שֵׁם אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב.

May the Lord answer you in the day of trouble; may the name of Jacob’s God protect you.

יִשְׁלַח עֶזְרְךָ מִקֹּֽדֶשׁ, וּמִצִּיּוֹן יִסְעָדֶֽךָּ.

May he send you help from the sanctuary, and give you support from Zion.

יִזְכֹּר כָּל מִנְחֹתֶֽךָ, וְעוֹלָתְךָ יְדַשְּׁנֶה סֶּֽלָה.

May he remember all of your offerings, and be pleased with all your sacrifices.

יִתֶּן־לְךָ כִלְבָבֶֽךָ, וְכָל עֲצָתְךָ יְמַלֵּא.

May he grant you your heart’s desire, and fulfill all your plans.

נְרַנְּנָה בִּישׁוּעָתֶֽךָ, וּבְשֵׁם אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ נִדְגֹּל; יְמַלֵּא יְיָ כָּל מִשְׁאֲלוֹתֶֽיךָ.

We will exult over your victory, and raise our banners in the name of God; may he fulfill all your requests.

עַתָּה יָדַֽעְתִּי כִּי הוֹשִֽׁיעַ יְיָ מְשִׁיחוֹ, יַעֲנֵֽהוּ מִשְּׁמֵי קָדְשׁוֹ, בִּגְבֻרוֹת יֵֽשַׁע יְמִינוֹ.

Now I know that the Lord has saved his anointed, answering him from his holy heavens with the mighty acts of his saving power.

אֵֽלֶּה בָרֶֽכֶב וְאֵֽלֶּה בַסּוּסִים, וַאֲנַֽחְנוּ בְּשֵׁם יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ נַזְכִּיר.

Some trust in chariots, and some in horses, but we call upon the name of the Lord our God.

הֵֽמָּה כָּרְעוּ וְנָפָֽלוּ, וַאֲנַֽחְנוּ קָּֽמְנוּ וַנִּתְעוֹדָד.

They bend and fall; we rise and stand upright.

{reader}יְיָ הוֹשִֽׁיעָה; הַמֶּֽלֶךְ יַעֲנֵֽנוּ בְיוֹם קָרְאֵֽנוּ.

O Lord, save us; may the King answer us when we call.

### uva_letzion — Hebrew 131–133; English 132–134

וּבָא לְצִיּוֹן גּוֹאֵל, וּלְשָׁבֵי פֶֽשַׁע בְּיַעֲקֹב, נְאֻם יְיָ.

A redeemer shall come to Zion and to those in Jacob who turn from transgression, says the Lord.

וַאֲנִי, זֹאת בְּרִיתִי אוֹתָם, אָמַר יְיָ: רוּחִי אֲשֶׁר עָלֶֽיךָ, וּדְבָרַי אֲשֶׁר שַֽׂמְתִּי בְּפִֽיךָ לֹא יָמֽוּשׁוּ מִפִּֽיךָ וּמִפִּי זַרְעֲךָ, וּמִפִּי זֶֽרַע זַרְעֲךָ, אָמַר יְיָ, מֵעַתָּה וְעַד עוֹלָם.

As for me, this is my covenant with them, says the Lord: My spirit it is which shall be upon you; and my words which I have put in your mouth shall not depart from your mouth, nor from the mouth of your children, nor from the mouth of your children’s children, says the Lord, henceforth and forever.

וְאַתָּה קָדוֹשׁ, יוֹשֵׁב תְּהִלּוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל.

{p}Thou, holy God, art enthroned amidst the praises of Israel.

וְקָרָא זֶה אֶל זֶה וְאָמַר: קָדוֹשׁ, קָדוֹשׁ, קָדוֹשׁ יְיָ צְבָאוֹת; מְלֹא כָל הָאָֽרֶץ כְּבוֹדוֹ.

They keep calling to one another: “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory.”

וּמְקַבְּלִין דֵּין מִן דֵּין וְאָמְרִין: קַדִּישׁ בִּשְׁמֵי מְרוֹמָא עִלָּאָה, בֵּית שְׁכִינְתֵּהּ; קַדִּישׁ עַל אַרְעָא, עוֹבַד גְּבוּרְתֵּהּ; קַדִּישׁ לְעָלַם וּלְעָלְמֵי עָלְמַיָּא; יְיָ צְבָאוֹת; מַלְיָא כָל אַרְעָא זִיו יְקָרֵהּ.

They receive it from one another, and say: “Holy in the highest heavens, his divine abode; holy upon earth, his work of might; holy forever and to all eternity is the Lord of hosts; the whole earth is full of his radiant glory.”

{p}וַתִּשָּׂאֵֽנִי רֽוּחַ, וָאֶשְׁמַע אַחֲרַי קוֹל רַֽעַשׁ גָּדוֹל: בָּרוּךְ כְּבוֹד יְיָ מִמְּקוֹמוֹ.

Then a wind lifted me up, and I heard behind me a mighty sound: “Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his abode.”

וּנְטָלַֽתְנִי רוּחָא, וּשְׁמָעִית בַּתְרַי קָל זִֽיעַ שַׂגִּיא דִּמְשַׁבְּחִין וְאָמְרִין: בְּרִיךְ יְקָרָא דַיְיָ מֵאֲתַר בֵּית שְׁכִינְתֵּהּ.

Then a wind lifted me up and I heard behind me a great moving sound of those who uttered praises, saying: “Blessed be the glory of the Lord from the {pb:134}place of his divine abode.”

יְיָ {pb:133}יִמְלֹךְ לְעֹלָם וָעֶד.

The Lord shall reign forever and ever.

יְיָ מַלְכוּתֵהּ (קָאֵם) לְעָלַם וּלְעָלְמֵי עָלְמַיָּא.

The Lord’s kingship is established forever and to all eternity.

{p}יְיָ אֱלֹהֵי אַבְרָהָם יִצְחָק וְיִשְׂרָאֵל אֲבוֹתֵֽינוּ, שָׁמְרָה זֹּאת לְעוֹלָם, לְיֵֽצֶר מַחְשְׁבוֹת לְבַב עַמֶּֽךָ, וְהָכֵן לְבָבָם אֵלֶֽיךָ.

{p}Lord God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel our fathers, keep the mind and purpose of thy people ever in this spirit, and direct their heart to thyself.

וְהוּא רַחוּם, יְכַפֵּר עָוֹן וְלֹא יַשְׁחִית, וְהִרְבָּה לְהָשִׁיב אַפּוֹ, וְלֹא יָעִיר כָּל חֲמָתוֹ.

He, being merciful, forgives iniquity, and does not destroy; frequently he turns his anger away, and does not stir up all his wrath.

כִּי אַתָּה, אֲדֹנָי, טוֹב וְסַלָּח, וְרַב חֶֽסֶד לְכָל קֹרְאֶֽיךָ.

For thou, O Lord, art good and forgiving, and exceedingly kind to all who call upon thee.

צִדְקָתְךָ צֶֽדֶק לְעוֹלָם, וְתוֹרָתְךָ אֱמֶת.

Thy righteousness is eternal, and thy Torah is truth.

תִּתֵּן אֱמֶת לְיַעֲקֹב, חֶֽסֶד לְאַבְרָהָם, אֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּֽעְתָּ לַאֲבֹתֵֽינוּ מִֽימֵי קֶֽדֶם.

Thou wilt show grace to Jacob, love to Abraham, as thou hast sworn to our fathers from days of old.

בָּרוּךְ יְיָ, יוֹם יוֹם יַעֲמָס־לָֽנוּ; הָאֵל יְשׁוּעָתֵֽנוּ, סֶֽלָה.

Blessed be the Lord who day by day bears our burden; God is ever our salvation.

יְיָ צְבָאוֹת עִמָּֽנוּ, מִשְׂגָּב־לָֽנוּ אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב, סֶֽלָה.

The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our stronghold.

יְיָ צְבָאוֹת, אַשְׁרֵי אָדָם בֹּטֵֽחַ בָּךְ.

Lord of hosts, happy is the man who trusts in thee.

יְיָ הוֹשִֽׁיעָה; הַמֶּֽלֶךְ יַעֲנֵֽנוּ בְיוֹם קָרְאֵֽנוּ.

O Lord, save us; may the King answer us when we call.

Such is the fate of those who trust in themselves, and the end of those who are pleased with their own mouthing.

כַּצֹּאן לִשְׁאוֹל שַֽׁתּוּ, מָֽוֶת יִרְעֵם; וַיִּרְדּוּ בָם יְשָׁרִים לַבֹּֽקֶר, וְצוּרָם לְבַלּוֹת שְׁאוֹל {pb:151}מִזְּבֻל לוֹ.

Like sheep they are destined to die; death shall shepherd them; the upright shall triumph over them in the morning; their form is to decay in the nether-world, {pb:152}its habitation.

אַךְ אֱלֹהִים יִפְדֶּה נַפְשִׁי מִיַּד שְׁאוֹל, כִּי יִקָּחֵֽנִי סֶֽלָה.

However, God will release me from the grasp of death, for he will receive me.

אַל תִּירָא כִּי יַעֲשִׁר אִישׁ, כִּי יִרְבֶּה כְּבוֹד בֵּיתוֹ.

So fear not when a man grows rich, when the splendor of his house increases;

כִּי לֹא בְמוֹתוֹ יִקַּח הַכֹּל, לֹא יֵרֵד אַחֲרָיו כְּבוֹדוֹ.

for he will take nothing with him when he dies; his wealth will not follow him below.

כִּי נַפְשׁוֹ בְּחַיָּיו יְבָרֵךְ; וְיוֹדֻֽךָ כִּי תֵיטִיב לָךְ.

Though while he lives he flatters himself [saying]: “Men acclaim you when you do well for yourself,”

תָּבוֹא עַד דּוֹר אֲבוֹתָיו, עַד נֵֽצַח לֹא יִרְאוּ אוֹר.

he will join the generation of his fathers, who will never see the light.

{reader}אָדָם בִּיקָר וְלֹא יָבִין, נִמְשַׁל כַּבְּהֵמוֹת נִדְמוּ.

The man who lives in splendor and understands not is like the beasts that perish.

## Sunday Psalm 24 — Hebrew 139; English 140

לְדָוִד מִזְמוֹר. לַיְיָ הָאָֽרֶץ וּמְלוֹאָהּ, תֵּבֵל וְיֹֽשְׁבֵי בָהּ.

A psalm of David. The earth and its entire contents belong to the Lord, the world and its inhabitants.

כִּי הוּא עַל יַמִּים יְסָדָהּ, וְעַל נְהָרוֹת יְכוֹנְנֶֽהָ.

For it is he who has founded it upon the seas, and established it on the floods.

מִי יַעֲלֶה בְהַר יְיָ, וּמִי יָקוּם בִּמְקוֹם קָדְשׁוֹ.

Who may ascend the Lord’s mountain? Who may stand within his holy place?

נְקִי כַפַּֽיִם וּבַר לֵבָב, אֲשֶׁר לֹא נָשָׂא לַשָּׁוְא נַפְשִׁי, וְלֹא נִשְׁבַּע לְמִרְמָה.

He who has clean hands and a pure heart; he who strives not after vanity and swears not deceitfully.

יִשָּׂא בְרָכָה מֵאֵת יְיָ, וּצְדָקָה מֵאֱלֹהֵי יִשְׁעוֹ.

He will receive a blessing from the Lord, and justice from his saving God.

זֶה דּוֹר דֹּרְשָׁיו, מְבַקְשֵׁי פָנֶֽיךָ יַעֲקֹב, סֶֽלָה.

Such is the generation of those who are in quest of him, who seek the presence of the God of Jacob.

שְׂאוּ שְׁעָרִים רָאשֵׁיכֶם, וְהִנָּשְׂאוּ פִּתְחֵי עוֹלָם, וְיָבוֹא מֶֽלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד.

Raise your heads, O gates, raise yourselves, you ancient doors, that the glorious King may come in.

מִי זֶה מֶֽלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד, יְיָ עִזּוּז וְגִבּוֹר, יְיָ גִּבּוֹר מִלְחָמָה.

Who, then, is the glorious King? The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord strong in battle.

שְׂאוּ שְׁעָרִים רָאשֵׁיכֶם, וּשְׂאוּ פִּתְחֵי עוֹלָם, וְיָבֹא מֶֽלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד.

Raise your heads, O gates, raise yourselves, you ancient doors, that the glorious King may come in.

{reader}מִי הוּא זֶה מֶֽלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד, יְיָ צְבָאוֹת הוּא מֶֽלֶךְ הַכָּבוֹד, סֶֽלָה.

Who, then, is the glorious King? The Lord of hosts, he is the glorious King.

## Printed notes

128, citation 3: Psalms 84:5; 144:15.

130, citation 1: Psalm 115:18. These repeat the citations already present with the earlier Ashrei.

### commentary: ובא לציון

which consists of biblical passages accompanied by the paraphrase of the Targum, was designed to enable every Jew to have a daily share in the study of the Torah (Rashi, Sotah 49a). On Sabbaths and festivals, when the Torah and the Prophets are read at great length, the recitation of this collection of Scriptural passages is postponed till the afternoon service.

### commentary: עלינו

is the proclamation of God as King over a united humanity. An old tradition claims Joshua as its author. Taken from the Musaf service of Rosh Hashanah, Alenu has been used as the closing prayer of the daily services since the thirteenth century. It is reported that it was the death-song of Jewish martyrs in the Middle Ages. Alenu has been the occasion of repeated attacks on account of the passage: “They bow to vanity and emptiness and pray to a god that cannot save” (שהם משתחוים להבל וריק ומתפללים אל אל לא יושיע). Through fear of the official censors, the passage in question has been excluded from the prayer.

### commentary: שיר של יום

the Psalm of the Day, was chanted by the Levites each day during the Temple service (Mishnah Tamid 7:4). According to the Talmud, the daily psalms were intended to recall the incidents of the six days of creation (Rosh Hashanah 31a).

### commentary: מזמור

a poem sung to the accompaniment of musical instruments in the Temple service.

### commentary: שאו שערים ראשיכם

The ancient gates of Zion are poetically commanded to raise their heads, in token of reverence to God whose entrance is an act of condescension. Different parts of this psalm were sung by different choirs of singers at the time when David brought the ark to Mount Zion.

### commentary: בני קרח

descendants of Korah, a division of Levites who sang in the Temple.

### commentary: המה ראו

they saw the impregnable might of Zion and were terrified.

### commentary: אניות תרשיש

the great seagoing vessels that made the long voyage to Tarshish, a seacoast city in Spain (or Carthage).

### commentary: כאשר שמענו כן ראינו

that is, history has repeated itself. We have now experienced events similar to those which occurred in the past. This psalm celebrates the escape of Jerusalem from a threatened invasion by the armies of various confederate kings.

### commentary: סובו ציון…

that is, after the miraculous deliverance of Zion, its inhabitants can now freely walk around and contemplate the safety of the walls and towers and palaces so lately menaced with destruction.

### commentary: נצב בעדת אל…

God takes his stand in the assembly summoned by him, and denounces the wickedness and partiality of judges. He reminds them of their duties, and declares that because they are ignorant and corrupt human society is undermined.

### commentary: אני אמרתי…

I appointed you as judges and thus invested you with authority of administering divine justice; however, your high title will not exempt you from punishment. You shall die like common men, and fall like any other prince.

### commentary: קומה

The psalmist pleads that God should act as judge over all peoples, since the human judges have failed so miserably.

### commentary: אל נקמות

is repeated for emphasis. The psalmist appeals to God to punish the arrogant who contemptuously declare that God is indifferent to the sufferings of his people. He then turns to argue with those who foolishly agree with their oppressors and think that God will not defend them. He who gave others the power to hear and see can surely himself hear and see. God knows the evil thoughts of the wicked, and eventually the righteous will be vindicated when the day of retribution comes. It is unthinkable that God would abandon his people to the ravages of lawless judges and tyrannical rulers.

### commentary: למנצח

occurs in the titles of fifty-five psalms, and refers to the use of the psalm in the Temple services. The word means the conductor of the Temple choir, who trained the choir and led the music.

### commentary: על הגתית

occurs in the titles of three psalms. According to the Targum, Gittith was a harp used by the Philistines of Gath. Since the Hebrew word gath means “a winepress,” Gittith may mean a melody sung at vintage festivals.

### commentary: בחדש

is rendered by the Targum and the Talmud: Rosh Ḥodesh Tishri, that is Rosh Hashanah. Metal trumpets, and not a shofar, were used on all other occasions of Rosh Ḥodesh.

### commentary: בכסה ליום חגנו

that is, the Sukkoth festival which begins on the fifteenth of Tishri when the moon is full.

### commentary: יהוסף

is a synonym for Israel, so called from the favored son of Israel. In Psalm 77:16, Jacob and Joseph are named as the fathers of the entire people of Israel.

### commentary: שפת לא ידעתי…

The psalmist represents Israel as quoting the following words of God, heard for the first time after the exodus from Egypt.

### commentary: על מי מריבה

refers to Exodus 17:7; Numbers 20:13.

### commentary: הרחב פיך…

God will abundantly supply your needs as long as you are faithful to him.

### commentary: משנאי ה׳…

God’s enemies are the enemies of his people, and he would compel them to pay homage to Israel. Israel’s national existence and prosperity would know no end.

### commentary: גאות לבש…

The psalmist speaks of God’s attributes as a glorious garment wrapped about him. God’s rule reestablishes the moral order of the world. Rashi and others interpret this psalm in connection with the Messianic era.

### commentary: נשאו נהרות…

God’s control of the violent forces of nature is used here to represent his power over the mighty enemies of his people.

### commentary: עדותיך…

God’s moral laws are firmly established and unchangeable. Zion, his house, shall no longer be desecrated by heathen invaders.

### commentary: ה׳ אורי וישעי

The first part of this psalm expresses fearless confidence in the face of hostile armies, while the second part is a prayer of one in deep distress and beset by false accusers.

### commentary: לאכל את בשרי

to eat my flesh, like wild beasts of prey.

### commentary: שבתי בבית ה׳

that is, living securely under God’s protection and enjoying his hospitality.

### commentary: לך אמר לבי…

The psalmist, in his heart, quotes God’s command to the effect that all must seek access to his presence.

### commentary: אבי ואמי עזבוני…

Though I am orphaned, friendless and deserted, God will be father to me and protect me.

### commentary: לולא האמנתי…

The remainder of the sentence is left to the imagination: “What would my condition be, if I had not believed!”

### commentary: שמעו זאת…

The psalmist addresses all the inhabitants of the world and summons them to hear his parable which concerns all humanity.

### commentary: חכמות

moral philosophy. The rich man cannot deliver his friends or himself from death, and his prosperity need cause no dismay to those who are less fortunate.

### commentary: קרבם

They delude themselves with the thought that their names will be perpetuated in the names of their estates.

### commentary: מות ירעם…

death will take control of them; and in the morning, when the dark night of suffering is over, the victims of lawlessness will be triumphant over their fallen oppressors.

### commentary: אך…יפדה נפשי

The psalmist is confident that God will deliver him from the premature death of the wicked and will receive him under his divine protection.
