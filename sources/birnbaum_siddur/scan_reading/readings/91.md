# Printed page 91 (Hebrew; scan 116, leaf 115)

## THE MECHANISM THIS PAGE INTRODUCES: two SIMULTANEOUS columns, split by a vertical rule

Modim and Modim d'Rabbanan stand side by side, divided by a printed VERTICAL RULE.
This is NOT the alternation of page 87. Both are said — the Reader says the right
column aloud while the Congregation says the left one. They are simultaneous, and
encoding them as alternatives would be wrong.

    RIGHT column (the wider one, no heading): מוֹדִים — said by all / by the Reader
    LEFT column, headed:
        When the Reader repeats the Shemoneh Esreh, the Congregation responds here by
        saying:
    and set in parentheses: (מוֹדִים אֲנַֽחְנוּ לָךְ ... בָּרוּךְ אֵל הַהוֹדָאוֹת.)

*** WHICH ONE COMES FIRST WHEN THE COLUMNS ARE LINEARISED: MODIM D'RABBANAN. ***

The two columns begin at the same vertical position, so "responds **here**" points at the
moment Modim begins. Printed without columns, the conditional passage has to come FIRST:
put Modim first and "here" lands at the *end* of Modim, which is not where the
congregation starts saying it. There is no way to set simultaneity in a single column, so
the order is chosen to keep the instruction's deixis true, and that is the only thing
choosing it can preserve.

    בְּרַחֲמֶֽיךָ הָרַבִּים עָמַֽדְתָּ לָהֶם
    [continues on page 93]

Note the em-dash after בַּזְּמַן הַזֶּה and the paragraph break after it: Al HaNissim is
two paragraphs, the second beginning בִּימֵי. The parenthesis opens here and closes on 93.

The rubric names Ḥanukkah alone. The Purim Al HaNissim is printed too, but SEPARATELY,
on page 93 under its own rubric "On Purim add:" — two complete passages, not one passage
with a variable middle. Each has its own parentheses and its own opening formula.
