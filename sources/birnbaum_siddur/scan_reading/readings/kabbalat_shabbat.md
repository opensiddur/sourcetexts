## Order and rubrics

237/238: קַבָּלַת שַׁבָּת / WELCOMING THE SABBATH

The following Kabbalath Shabbath service is omitted on festivals which coincide with the Sabbath. The Ma‘ariv service for festivals begins on page 257 [English: 258].

Psalms 95, 96, 97, 98, 99, 29; Ana Bekoach and Barukh Shem; Lecha Dodi; welcome for mourners; Psalms 92 and 93; Mourners’ Kaddish.

Hebrew Reader cues begin at 95:10, 96:13, 97:11, 98:9, 99:8, 29:10, 92:15, and 93:5. The English does not repeat these cues.

243/244 before Lecha Dodi: Reader and Congregation:

245/246: Each of the following stanzas is recited first by the Congregation and then by the Reader.

247/248 before the final stanza: Congregation rises and turns toward the door, as if to welcome a guest.

There is a custom that mourners, in the first week of their mourning, remain at the entrance of the synagogue until one of the congregation says to them:

הַמָּקוֹם יְנַחֵם אֶתְכֶם בְּתוֹךְ שְׁאָר אֲבֵלֵי צִיּוֹן וִירוּשָׁלָיִם.

May God console you among the other mourners for Zion and Jerusalem.

247/248 before Psalm 92: If a festival occurs on Friday, the evening service begins here.

## Encoding decisions

- The incoming Hebrew date governs the Shabbat caller. A festival on Shabbat omits the whole service in its caller. A festival on the preceding Friday omits the portion before Psalm 92. Location distinguishes the last festival day in Israel and the Diaspora. Unknown calendar settings leave the rubrics and MAYBE content visible.
- Mourners present is an independent, initially unknown service-context setting; it is not the house-of-mourning setting. The concluding Kaddish requires a minyan.
- All eight psalms use biblical URNs. Earlier isolated verses from Psalms 29, 95 and 99 retain their printed wording and local excerpt milestones with biblical source spans, while the complete chapters own the biblical verse addresses. Notably, Psalm 95:3 translates אלהים as “gods” here but “powers” in the Wednesday excerpt.
- Psalms 95–99 and 29 have one English paragraph per printed line group, not necessarily per biblical verse. English 95:9, 96:10, 96:13, and 98:9 contain paragraph breaks inside a verse. Psalm 92 is prose in both languages.
- The Hebrew opening Lecha Dodi refrain is printed twice on 243, while its English is printed once on 244. All nine stanzas have a following refrain in both languages. Hebrew stanzas have four hemistichs in two printed rows; the encoding stores four lines. Each stanza/refrain has a milestone correspondence; repeated refrains reference the canonical refrain.
- Ana Bekoach is shared with the morning service. Enlargement confirms ושמע in its seventh line, exactly as in the earlier reading. Barukh Shem and Mourners’ Kaddish are also shared. Psalm 93 repeats the earlier Friday psalm, with the same words and pointing; reuse retains the earlier punctuation (comma rather than the semicolon after “old” in 93:2) and stress marks.
- All thirteen commentary paragraphs are retained in twelve entries: the two paragraphs on Psalm 93 share one occurrence-specific annotation. Continued paragraphs on 237–238, 243–244, and 247–248 are joined.

### Psalm 95 (printed 237–238)

Hebrew:

1. לְכוּ נְרַנְּנָה לַייָ, נָרִיעָה לְצוּר יִשְׁעֵנוּ.
2. נְקַדְּמָה פָנָיו בְּתוֹדָה, בִּזְמִרוֹת נָרִיעַ לוֹ.
3. כִּי אֵל גָּדוֹל יְיָ, וּמֶלֶךְ גָּדוֹל עַל כָּל אֱלֹהִים.
4. אֲשֶׁר בְּיָדוֹ מֶחְקְרֵי אָרֶץ, וְתוֹעֲפוֹת הָרִים לוֹ.
5. אֲשֶׁר לוֹ הַיָּם, וְהוּא עָשָׂהוּ; וְיַבֶּשֶׁת יָדָיו יָצָרוּ.
6. בֹּאוּ נִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה וְנִכְרָעָה, נִבְרְכָה לִפְנֵי יְיָ עֹשֵׂנוּ.
7. כִּי הוּא אֱלֹהֵינוּ, וַאֲנַחְנוּ עַם מַרְעִיתוֹ וְצֹאן יָדוֹ; הַיּוֹם אִם בְּקֹלוֹ תִשְׁמָעוּ.
8. אַל תַּקְשׁוּ לְבַבְכֶם כִּמְרִיבָה, כְּיוֹם מַסָּה בַּמִּדְבָּר.
9. אֲשֶׁר נִסּוּנִי אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם; בְּחָנוּנִי גַּם רָאוּ פָעֳלִי.
10. אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה אָקוּט בְּדוֹר, וָאֹמַר עַם תֹּעֵי לֵבָב הֵם, וְהֵם לֹא יָדְעוּ דְרָכָי.
11. אֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי בְאַפִּי, אִם יְבֹאוּן אֶל מְנוּחָתִי.

English:

1. Come, let us sing to the Lord; let us acclaim our saving Stronghold.
2. Let us approach him with thanksgiving; let us acclaim him with songs of praise.
3. For the Lord is a great God, a King supreme above all gods.
4. In his hand are the depths of the earth; the mountain-peaks are his.
5. His is the sea, for he made it; his hands formed the dry land.
6. Come, let us worship and bow down; let us bend the knee before the Lord who made us.
7. For he is our God, and we are the people he sustains, the flock under his charge; if this day you would only obey his voice!
8. Harden not your heart as at Meribah, as in the day of Massah in the wilderness,
9. when your fathers tried me. {p}They tested me, although they had seen my work.
10. For forty years I loathed that generation, and said: “They are a senseless people, who know not my ways.”
11. So I vowed in my anger that they should never enter my land.

### Psalm 96 (printed 237–240)

Hebrew:

1. שִׁירוּ לַייָ שִׁיר חָדָשׁ, שִׁירוּ לַייָ כָּל הָאָרֶץ.
2. שִׁירוּ לַייָ בָּרְכוּ שְׁמוֹ, בַּשְּׂרוּ מִיּוֹם לְיוֹם יְשׁוּעָתוֹ.
3. סַפְּרוּ בַגּוֹיִם כְּבוֹדוֹ, בְּכָל הָעַמִּים נִפְלְאוֹתָיו.
4. כִּי גָדוֹל יְיָ וּמְהֻלָּל מְאֹד, נוֹרָא הוּא עַל כָּל אֱלֹהִים.
5. כִּי כָּל אֱלֹהֵי הָעַמִּים אֱלִילִים, וַייָ שָׁמַיִם עָשָׂה.
6. הוֹד {pb:239}וְהָדָר לְפָנָיו, עֹז וְתִפְאֶרֶת בְּמִקְדָּשׁוֹ.
7. הָבוּ לַייָ, מִשְׁפְּחוֹת עַמִּים, הָבוּ לַייָ כָּבוֹד וָעֹז.
8. הָבוּ לַייָ כְּבוֹד שְׁמוֹ, שְׂאוּ מִנְחָה וּבֹאוּ לְחַצְרוֹתָיו.
9. הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַייָ בְּהַדְרַת קֹדֶשׁ, חִילוּ מִפָּנָיו כָּל הָאָרֶץ.
10. אִמְרוּ בַגּוֹיִם יְיָ מָלָךְ, אַף תִּכּוֹן תֵּבֵל בַּל תִּמּוֹט; יָדִין עַמִּים בְּמֵישָׁרִים.
11. יִשְׂמְחוּ הַשָּׁמַיִם וְתָגֵל הָאָרֶץ; יִרְעַם הַיָּם וּמְלֹאוֹ.
12. יַעֲלֹז שָׂדַי וְכָל אֲשֶׁר בּוֹ; אָז יְרַנְּנוּ כָּל עֲצֵי יָעַר.
13. לִפְנֵי יְיָ כִּי בָא, כִּי בָא לִשְׁפֹּט הָאָרֶץ; יִשְׁפֹּט תֵּבֵל בְּצֶדֶק, וְעַמִּים בֶּאֱמוּנָתוֹ.

English:

1. Sing a new song to the Lord; sing to the Lord, all the earth.
2. Sing to the Lord, bless his name; announce his salvation from day to day.
3. Recount his glory among the nations, his wonders—among the peoples.
4. For great is the Lord and highly to be praised; he is to be revered above all gods.
5. For all the gods of the peoples are idols, but the Lord made the heavens.
6. {pb:240}Grandeur and majesty are before him; glory and beauty are in his sanctuary.
7. Ascribe to the Lord, O families of peoples, ascribe to the Lord glory and majesty.
8. Ascribe to the Lord the glory due to his name; bring an offering and come into his courts.
9. Worship the Lord in holy array; tremble before him, all the earth.
10. Say among the nations: “The Lord is King!” {p}The world is so established that it cannot be shaken; he rules the peoples justly.
11. Let the heavens rejoice, let the earth be glad, let the sea and all its fulness roar praise.
12. Let the field exult and all that is therein; let all the trees of the forest sing
13. before the Lord who comes, who comes to rule the earth. {p}He will rule the world with righteousness, and the peoples—with his truth.

### Psalm 97 (printed 239–240)

Hebrew:

1. יְיָ מָלָךְ, תָּגֵל הָאָרֶץ; יִשְׂמְחוּ אִיִּים רַבִּים.
2. עָנָן וַעֲרָפֶל סְבִיבָיו, צֶדֶק וּמִשְׁפָּט מְכוֹן כִּסְאוֹ.
3. אֵשׁ לְפָנָיו תֵּלֵךְ, וּתְלַהֵט סָבִיב צָרָיו.
4. הֵאִירוּ בְרָקָיו תֵּבֵל, רָאֲתָה וַתָּחֵל הָאָרֶץ.
5. הָרִים כַּדּוֹנַג נָמַסּוּ מִלִּפְנֵי יְיָ, מִלִּפְנֵי אֲדוֹן כָּל הָאָרֶץ.
6. הִגִּידוּ הַשָּׁמַיִם צִדְקוֹ, וְרָאוּ כָל הָעַמִּים כְּבוֹדוֹ.
7. יֵבֹשׁוּ כָּל עֹבְדֵי פֶסֶל, הַמִּתְהַלְלִים בָּאֱלִילִים; הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לוֹ כָּל אֱלֹהִים.
8. שָׁמְעָה וַתִּשְׂמַח צִיּוֹן, וַתָּגֵלְנָה בְּנוֹת יְהוּדָה, לְמַעַן מִשְׁפָּטֶיךָ יְיָ.
9. כִּי אַתָּה יְיָ עֶלְיוֹן עַל כָּל הָאָרֶץ; מְאֹד נַעֲלֵיתָ עַל כָּל אֱלֹהִים.
10. אֹהֲבֵי יְיָ שִׂנְאוּ רָע; שֹׁמֵר נַפְשׁוֹת חֲסִידָיו, מִיַּד רְשָׁעִים יַצִּילֵם.
11. אוֹר זָרֻעַ לַצַּדִּיק, וּלְיִשְׁרֵי לֵב שִׂמְחָה.
12. שִׂמְחוּ צַדִּיקִים בַּייָ, וְהוֹדוּ לְזֵכֶר קָדְשׁוֹ.

English:

1. The Lord is King; let the earth rejoice; let many islands be glad.
2. Clouds and darkness are around him; righteousness and justice are the foundation of his throne.
3. Fire goes before him, and burns his foes round about.
4. His lightnings illuminate the world; the earth beholds and trembles.
5. The mountains melt like wax before the Lord, before the Lord of all the earth.
6. The heavens proclaim his righteousness, and all the peoples witness his glory.
7. Ashamed be those who serve images, those who take pride in idols; bow down to him, all you gods!
8. Zion hears and is glad, and the towns of Judah rejoice, because of thy judgments, O Lord.
9. Thou, O Lord, art indeed supreme over all the earth; thou art exalted high above all gods.
10. You who love the Lord, hate evil! He preserves the lives of his faithful followers; he rescues them from the hand of the wicked.
11. Light is sown for the righteous, and joy for the upright in heart.
12. Rejoice in the Lord, you righteous, and give thanks to his holy name.

### Psalm 98 (printed 241–242)

Hebrew:

1. מִזְמוֹר, שִׁירוּ לַייָ שִׁיר חָדָשׁ, כִּי נִפְלָאוֹת עָשָׂה, הוֹשִׁיעָה לּוֹ יְמִינוֹ וּזְרוֹעַ קָדְשׁוֹ.
2. הוֹדִיעַ יְיָ יְשׁוּעָתוֹ, לְעֵינֵי הַגּוֹיִם גִּלָּה צִדְקָתוֹ.
3. זָכַר חַסְדּוֹ וֶאֱמוּנָתוֹ לְבֵית יִשְׂרָאֵל; רָאוּ כָל אַפְסֵי אָרֶץ אֵת יְשׁוּעַת אֱלֹהֵינוּ.
4. הָרִיעוּ לַייָ, כָּל הָאָרֶץ, פִּצְחוּ וְרַנְּנוּ וְזַמֵּרוּ.
5. זַמְּרוּ לַייָ בְּכִנּוֹר, בְּכִנּוֹר וְקוֹל זִמְרָה.
6. בַּחֲצֹצְרוֹת וְקוֹל שׁוֹפָר, הָרִיעוּ לִפְנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ יְיָ.
7. יִרְעַם הַיָּם וּמְלֹאוֹ, תֵּבֵל וְיֹשְׁבֵי בָהּ.
8. נְהָרוֹת יִמְחֲאוּ כָף, יַחַד הָרִים יְרַנֵּנוּ.
9. לִפְנֵי יְיָ כִּי בָא לִשְׁפֹּט הָאָרֶץ; יִשְׁפֹּט תֵּבֵל בְּצֶדֶק, וְעַמִּים בְּמֵישָׁרִים.

English:

1. Sing a new song to the Lord, for he has done wonders; his right hand, his holy arm, has brought him triumph.
2. The Lord has made known his saving power; he has let the nations see his justice.
3. He has remembered his kindness and faithfulness to the house of Israel; all the ends of the earth have seen the saving power of our God.
4. Shout praise to the Lord, all the earth; break into music, be jubilant and sing.
5. Praise the Lord with the harp, with the harp and the voice of song.
6. With trumpets and the sound of the horn, shout praise before the King, the Lord.
7. Let the sea and all its fulness thunder praise, the world and those living in it.
8. Let the rivers applaud, let the mountains sing in chorus,
9. before the Lord who comes to rule the earth! {p}He will rule the world with righteousness, and the peoples with justice.

### Psalm 99 (printed 241–242)

Hebrew:

1. יְיָ מָלָךְ, יִרְגְּזוּ עַמִּים; יֹשֵׁב כְּרוּבִים, תָּנוּט הָאָרֶץ.
2. יְיָ בְּצִיּוֹן גָּדוֹל, וְרָם הוּא עַל כָּל הָעַמִּים.
3. יוֹדוּ שִׁמְךָ, גָּדוֹל וְנוֹרָא; קָדוֹשׁ הוּא.
4. וְעֹז מֶלֶךְ מִשְׁפָּט אָהֵב, אַתָּה כּוֹנַנְתָּ מֵישָׁרִים; מִשְׁפָּט וּצְדָקָה בְּיַעֲקֹב אַתָּה עָשִׂיתָ.
5. רוֹמְמוּ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ, וְהִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַהֲדֹם רַגְלָיו; קָדוֹשׁ הוּא.
6. מֹשֶׁה וְאַהֲרֹן בְּכֹהֲנָיו, וּשְׁמוּאֵל בְּקֹרְאֵי שְׁמוֹ, קֹרִאים אֶל יְיָ, וְהוּא יַעֲנֵם.
7. בְּעַמּוּד עָנָן יְדַבֵּר אֲלֵיהֶם; שָׁמְרוּ עֵדֹתָיו וְחֹק נָתַן לָמוֹ.
8. יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ, אַתָּה עֲנִיתָם; אֵל נֹשֵׂא הָיִיתָ לָהֶם, וְנֹקֵם עַל עֲלִילוֹתָם.
9. רוֹמְמוּ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ, וְהִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לְהַר קָדְשׁוֹ, כִּי קָדוֹשׁ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ.

English:

1. The Lord is King; let the peoples tremble. He is enthroned upon the cherubim; let the earth quake.
2. The Lord is great in Zion; high is he above all the peoples.
3. Let them praise thy great and revered name; holy it is!
4. Thou, glorious King who lovest justice, hast established equity; thou hast wrought justice and righteousness in Jacob.
5. Exalt the Lord our God, and worship at his footstool; holy is he.
6. Moses and Aaron among his priests, and Samuel among those invoking his name, called upon the Lord, and he answered them.
7. Out of a pillar of cloud he spoke to them; they observed his precepts and the law which he gave them.
8. Lord our God, thou didst answer them; thou wast a forgiving God to them, though punishing them for their misdeeds.
9. Exalt the Lord our God, and worship at his holy mountain, for the Lord our God is holy.

### Psalm 29 (printed 241–244)

Hebrew:

1. מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד, הָבוּ לַייָ, בְּנֵי אֵלִים, הָבוּ לַייָ כָּבוֹד וָעֹז;
2. {pb:243}הָבוּ לַייָ כְּבוֹד שְׁמוֹ, הִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַייָ בְּהַדְרַת קֹדֶשׁ.
3. קוֹל יְיָ עַל הַמָּיִם, אֵל הַכָּבוֹד הִרְעִים, יְיָ עַל מַיִם רַבִּים.

## Commentary

### 1. קבלת שבת — urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/kabbalat_shabbat

the opening service on Friday evening, was introduced by the Kabbalists of the sixteenth century in Safed, Palestine. The six psalms, symbolizing the six working days of the week, were selected by Rabbi Moses Cordovero, whose brother-in-law Rabbi Solomon Alkabets composed the hymn welcoming the Sabbath bride (לכה דודי). The initial letters of the six psalms (ל, ש, י, מ, י, מ) have the numerical value of 430 which equals that of נפש (“soul”). מזמור לדוד (Psalm 29) contains the name of God eighteen times, a number corresponding to the eighteen blessings of the Shemoneh Esreh.

### 2.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/95

Psalm 95 is a call to worship the Creator of the world and Guardian of his people. It contains a warning against disobedience, and alludes to the fate of the rebellious Israelites in the wilderness (Exodus 17:7; Numbers 20:13).

### 3.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/96

Psalm 96 contrasts God’s power and glory with the worthlessness of the heathen idols, and appeals to the nations to acknowledge God and to rejoice in the prospect of his righteous rule on earth.

### 4.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/97

Psalm 97 is a mosaic of phrases borrowed from various Scriptural passages. It celebrates God’s manifestation of his sovereignty and calls his people to hate evil. All, even the gods existing in the minds of their worshipers, must do homage to the only true God. The Septuagint renders אור זרוע לצדיק in the sense of זרח . . . אור לישרים (Psalm 112:4): “Light dawns on the upright.”

### 5.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/98

Psalm 98 is largely made up of quotations, yet it is a stirring song of joyous praise. ירעם הים ומלאו, and the whole description of nature rejoicing at God’s coming to rule the world, is identical with Psalm 96:11–13. נהרות ימחאו כף is descriptive of the crashing of the waves.

### 6.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/99

Psalm 99 celebrates God’s universal sovereignty. His righteousness and faithfulness are manifested in the history of Israel. הדום רגליו refers to the ark (I Chronicles 28:2).

### 7.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/29

Psalm 29 describes the manifestation of God’s power in the thunderstorm and the flood, and ends with an assurance of his favor to his people.

### 8. אנא בכח — urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/kabbalat_shabbat/ana_bekhoach

a mystical meditation attributed to Rabbi Neḥunyah ben ha-Kanah, tanna of the second century.

### 9. לכה דודי — urn:x-opensiddur:text:poem:lecha_dodi

was written by Rabbi Solomon Alkabets about the middle of the sixteenth century. The name of the author, שלמה הלוי, is signed in the form of an acrostic at the beginning of the stanzas. This poem, “perhaps one of the finest pieces of religious poetry in existence,” became a favorite text of synagogal composers; a great number of melodies were set to it. Each stanza consists of four parts, three of which have the same rhyme, while the fourth part ends in the common rhyme לה throughout the poem. There is scarcely a phrase in the poem which is not borrowed from the Bible. Combining the language of the Bible into a rare mosaic, the poet utilized phrases from Isaiah 52:2; 51:17; 60:1; Judges 5:12; Isaiah 60:1; 54:4; Psalm 42:12; Isaiah 14:32; Jeremiah 30:18, 16; Isaiah 49:19; 62:5; 54:3; 25:9. The Sabbath is personified here and compared to a bride, in the same sense as Israel is likened to a bride (Jeremiah 2:2). The poem gives expression to the hope of Israel in vivid figures of speech.

### 10. שמור וזכור — urn:x-opensiddur:text:poem:lecha_dodi/1

refers to the talmudic explanation of the discrepancy between the two versions of the fourth commandment. In Exodus 20:8 the text reads: “Remember the Sabbath day,” and in Deuteronomy 5:12: “Observe the Sabbath day.” According to the Talmud (Shebuoth 20b), both words, זכור and שמור, were miraculously pronounced by God simultaneously.

### 11.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:bible:psalms/92

Psalm 92 was sung by the Levites in the Temple during the Sabbath offering. The psalmist reflects on the meaning of God’s works, a meaning which the foolish fail to perceive. The wicked seem to flourish only that they may be destroyed. The palm and cedar are long-lived and flourish during all seasons. They represent the enduring happiness of the faithful in contrast with the short-lived prosperity of the wicked.

### 12.  — urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/kabbalat_shabbat/psalm_93

Psalm 93 speaks of God’s majesty and power. His control of the violent forces of nature represents his power over the mightiest enemies of Israel. God’s rule is the security of all moral order in the world.

עדתיך נאמנו God’s moral laws are firmly established and unchangeable. Zion, his house, shall no longer be desecrated by heathen invaders. Rashi and others interpret this psalm in connection with the Messianic era.
