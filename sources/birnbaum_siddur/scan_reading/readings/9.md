# Printed page 9 (Hebrew; scan 34, leaf 33)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `9` at the top outer corner.

Opens mid-sentence, finishing the Yehi Ratzon begun on page 7, and closes mid-verse in
Exodus 13. Both breaks fall inside a `tei:p`.

**No footnote apparatus.** The first page of the unit with none at all — pages 3, 5 and 7
each carried at least one note. The foot is simply blank below the text.

## The page

Two things only: the tail of the Yehi Ratzon after putting on the tefillin, and then the
tefillin parashiyoth, opening with a centred Hebrew source citation.

    שמות יג, א–טז

Comma between chapter and verse, as page 5 sets its Psalm citation and unlike the facing
English page, which uses a colon. The convention holds.

The parashiyoth themselves are Exodus 13:1-16, set as continuous prose in two paragraphs
— `קַדֶּשׁ־לִי` and `וְהָיָה כִּי יְבִאֲךָ` — with no verse numbers and no headings between
them. The words are in `hebrew/9.txt` rather than repeated here; this page is scripture
set plain, and what is worth recording about it is the arrangement rather than the text.

**A maqqef in `קַדֶּשׁ־לִי`**, where the transcription sets a space. `compare` counts that
as whitespace, which is right — it is a joining mark, not a point.

**The two portions are not divided.** He runs Exodus 13:1-10 and 13:11-16 together as two
paragraphs of one passage with nothing between them. The Wikisource text carries an
editorial note saying exactly this, and observing that the facing English translation does
divide them. Worth checking against page 10 when it is read: if the English parts them and
the Hebrew does not, the two columns need the parting named on both sides or they will
drift, as Mah Tovu does on pages 3 and 4.

## The comparison

Twenty-two differences, and the split is worth recording because it is the first page
where a rule did most of the work.

**Ten are qamats qatan** — the transcription writing U+05C7 where this print has the one
qamats glyph and nothing else. That is the documented interpretation, so all ten are
`print` and none of them needed a crop or a question.

**Twelve are open**, and they are in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`: nine turn on a meteg, two on the
letters rather than the points, and one on a shva. A biblical passage set from a
different exemplar produces metegs by the dozen, and they are exactly the class this
reading cannot settle at band magnification.
