- **`קַדִּישׁ דְּרַבָּנָן`**, a centred Hebrew heading, and the Kaddish beginning under it.

64 words. The words are in `hebrew/45.txt`.

## A speaker label in English, centred, on the Hebrew page

    Mourners:

Centred, roman, on its own line between the Hebrew heading and the first words of the
Kaddish. The Amidah's Hebrew pages set their speaker labels as **inline** small roman
capitals, and printed page 19 set one in the **margin**; this is a third placement. Three
pages, three placements, all the same vocabulary — so placement is read off each page and
never carried over from the last.

## Footnote apparatus: three things under one rule

1. Item **`13.`**, the last of the `ILLUSTRATIONS` list begun under page 41 — no lemma,
   just the number.
2. A lemma-keyed note, `קדיש דרבנן`: *"(Scholars' Kaddish) is recited after the reading of
   talmudic or midrashic passages. `על ישראל ועל רבנן` is a prayer for the welfare of the
   scholars."* — one note carrying **two** unpointed Hebrew lemmas, the second inside its
   own prose.
3. A new headed section, small capitals, centred: **`THE KADDISH`**, beginning a long essay
   that runs on to page 47 and beyond.

So one rule can be followed by the tail of one note, a whole second note, and the head of a
third. The rule separates the apparatus from the body; it does not separate notes from each
other.
