# Printed page 27 (Hebrew; scan 52, leaf 51)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `27`. Opens a new sub-unit and closes at the end of
a verse.

## The page

The **korbanot** begin here. Three passages, two of them under a printed citation:

- `שמות ל, יז–כא` — the laver, `וַיְדַבֵּר יְיָ אֶל מֹשֶׁה לֵּאמֹר: וְעָשִֽׂיתָ כִּיּוֹר נְחֹֽשֶׁת`.
- the Yehi Ratzon, `יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלְּפָנֶֽיךָ … קָרְבַּן הַתָּמִיד`, under no citation.
- `במדבר כח, א–ח` — the daily offering, `צַו אֶת בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל`, which the page turn cuts.

The words are in `hebrew/27.txt`.

## A citation is printed in the Hebrew column, and is not read into `hebrew/`

Both citations are set **in the Hebrew column**, centred, in small unpointed type, above
the passage they introduce: `שמות ל, יז–כא` and `במדבר כח, א–ח`. Chapter and verse are
parted by a **comma** and the verse range by an **en dash**, as the Hebrew side sets them
throughout, and as printed page 19 sets `בראשית כב, א–יט`.

They are recorded here and **not** in `hebrew/27.txt`, which is the convention every page
has followed: `hebrew/` holds the words of the prayers and nothing else, so headings and
citations live in the reading. The transcription slice leaves the edition's `מקור` spans
out for the same reason, and the two sides therefore agree.

No heading and no rubric on this page — `קָרְבָּנוֹת` is not printed here, so the sub-unit
opens without one.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

One note, keyed `וידבר . . . ועשית כיור`:

> According to the Talmud, God said: "Whenever they recite the order of sacrifices, I will
> deem it as if they offered them before me and I will forgive them all their sins"
> (Ta'anith 27b). The sacrificial system symbolized self-surrender and devotion to the will
> of God. The peace-offering with its communion-feast showed the idea of fellowship. It
> served to keep alive the sense of dependence on God for the natural blessings of life,
> while

It **runs across the opening**, breaking mid-sentence at `while` and finishing under page 28.

**A second way of writing a span lemma.** Printed page 25's covers its span with an en dash
(`קדש את שמך–ברבים`); this one uses a **spaced ellipsis**, three dots with spaces around
them: `וידבר . . . ועשית כיור`. Both mean the same thing — a lemma with two ends — so an
encoding of the lemma has to carry the printed form rather than normalise it.

## The comparison

Ten differences over 176 words, all settled for the print, and none needing a crop beyond
two.

**Eight are qamats qatan**, settled by rule. This page has more of them per word than any
read so far, which is what the korbanot's vocabulary brings: `לְרָחְצָה`, `חָק־עוֹלָם`,
`וְתִמְחָל`, `קָרְבַּן`, `קָרְבָּנִי` and three of `כָּל`.

**One is the combined shin-dot/holam**, and it is the first place the recorded decision
does visible work. The print sets `משֶׁה` with a single dot at the shin's upper right
serving as both the shin dot and the mem's holam; the reading writes the holam as its own
character, because dropping it changes what the word is. The edition is inconsistent about
this — it writes `מֹשֶׁה` in the two scriptural passages on this same page and `משֶׁה` in
the Yehi Ratzon — which is what makes the difference show up at all.

**One is a meteg the transcription has and the print does not**: `בְּנֵי שָׁנָה`. Checked
at 20×; the ב carries a dagesh and a shva, the נ a tsere, and there is no vertical stroke
anywhere in the word.
