92 words. The words are in `hebrew/47.txt`.

## His parentheses, and the Ten Days

`לְעֵֽלָּא (לְעֵֽלָּא)` — the doubled word for the Ten Days of Repentance, in **his**
parentheses, with no rubric naming the occasion. Parentheses are his and are set as printed;
what they mean has to be supplied by the encoding rather than read off the page.

## A navigational rubric closes the section

The last line of Hebrew type on the page is an **English rubric, centred**:

> On Sabbaths and on major festivals the service is continued on page 299.

It is not a speaker label and not an instruction about how to say something — it is a
pointer to another place in the book, and it is the thing that ends the section. Printed
page 41's footnote carries the same kind of reference (`on page 13`), so the book does this
in both the body and the apparatus.

Page 299 is outside what these projects hold, which makes the reference a dangling one for
now. Set it as printed; do not invent a link it cannot follow.

## Footnote apparatus

The `THE KADDISH` essay continues from page 45 and runs past this page: Rabbi Pool on the
Kaddish's date, `על הכל`, `צדוק הדין`, and a numbered list of the Kaddish's five forms
(`1) קדיש דרבנן`, `2) קדיש שלם`, `3) חצי קדיש`, `4) קדיש יתום`, cut mid-word at `Kad-`).
A **hyphenated word broken across the page** — the first in the unit.

Note the numbering: `1)` with a closing parenthesis, matching Rabbi Ishmael's rules on
page 41 and not the `1.` of the `ILLUSTRATIONS` list. Two numbered lists in one apparatus,
numbered differently.

## The comparison

Nine differences over 92 words, all settled for the print, none open.

**Five are qamats qatan**, recorded by the tool. The other four each needed the image:

- `תַּלְמִידֵיהוֹן` — the print has the dagesh in the tav, the edition does not. Checked at
  10×; the dot is plain.
- `הָדֵן` — the edition adds a comma the print does not set. The word ends the line and
  nothing follows it.
- `בְּכָל` — the edition drops the **beth's** dagesh as well as writing qamats qatan, so the
  rule does not cover it. At 15× the beth carries a dagesh and a shva and the kaf carries a
  plain qamats and no dagesh. Third page on which `כל` has needed the image.
- `וּפֻרְקָנָא` — **consonantal**: the print sets a qubbuts and the edition a full vav
  (`וּפוּרְקָנָא`). The first consonantal difference since printed 15.

## The transcription had to be fixed to read this page

The Kaddish is the one place in the foundation text where a `{{נוסח}}` template holds a
template of its own:

    {{נוסח|בְּאַתְרָא [{{ק|בארץ:}} קַדִּישָׁא]|בירנבוים=בְּאַתְרָא}}

The resolver closed on the **inner** `}}`, took half a word as the positional, threw the
`בירנבוים=` reading away, and left a stray `[` in the slice. It now scans balanced braces.
No earlier page changed, because this is the only occurrence — which is exactly why it went
unnoticed until the last page of the section.
