# Printed page 1 (Hebrew; scan 26, leaf 25)

The first page of the body. No running head — the book sets running heads from page 3
onward. The page number `1` is printed, centred, at the foot, below the footnote.

Foliation runs straight on from the front matter: the last front-matter leaf is scan 25.

## Heading (Hebrew, centred)

    שַׁחֲרִית לִילָדִים

The facing English page heads the same section MORNING PRAYER FOR CHILDREN. The two
headings are in different languages, unlike the Amidah, where the heading is English on
both sides.

## The page

Four rubrics, each set in **English** and centred, in the Hebrew column — the same
practice as the Amidah's pages. Each governs exactly one passage that follows it, except
the last, which governs two.

**They are set in roman here, and in italic on the facing English page.** Checked at 5x
against page 2, side by side.

### Rubric

    Upon awakening in the morning:

[Modeh Ani]

    מוֹדֶה אֲנִי לְפָנֶֽיךָ, מֶֽלֶךְ חַי וְקַיָּם, שֶׁהֶחֱזַֽרְתָּ בִּי נִשְׁמָתִי
    בְּחֶמְלָה; רַבָּה אֱמוּנָתֶֽךָ.

### Rubric

    When washing the hands:

[al netilat yadayim]

    בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, מֶֽלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָֽׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֺתָיו
    וְצִוָּֽנוּ עַל נְטִילַת יָדָֽיִם.

### Rubric

    When putting on the arba kanfoth:

`arba kanfoth` is set in **italic** within this roman rubric, so it stands out. The facing
English page does NOT do the same: there the whole rubric is italic and the term is
indistinguishable within it. Same intent — a transliterated Hebrew term, marked as
foreign — realised differently because an italic term inside an italic rubric has nowhere
to go. Both projects encode it as foreign and leave the rendering to the typography.

[al mitzvat tzitzit]

    בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, מֶֽלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָֽׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֺתָיו
    וְצִוָּֽנוּ עַל מִצְוַת צִיצִת.

Defective צִיצִת, not צִיצִית.

### Rubric

    When dressed:

This rubric governs **two** paragraphs, not one — the only rubric on the page that does.

[Torah tziva — a single run-on paragraph holding six distinct texts, with no visual
break between them beyond the sentence punctuation]

    תּוֹרָה צִוָּה לָֽנוּ מֹשֶׁה, מוֹרָשָׁה קְהִלַּת יַעֲקֹב. בְּרָכוֹת יָחֻֽלוּ עַל רֹאשִׁי.
    שְׁמַע בְּנִי מוּסַר אָבִֽיךָ, וְאַל תִּטֹּשׁ תּוֹרַת אִמֶּֽךָ. תּוֹרָה תְּהִי אֱמוּנָתִי,
    וְאֵל שַׁדַּי בְּעֶזְרָתִי. אֵל מֶֽלֶךְ נֶאֱמָן. שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ,
    יְיָ אֶחָד. בָּרוּךְ שֵׁם כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתוֹ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד. וְאַתֶּם הַדְּבֵקִים בַּייָ
    אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, חַיִּים כֻּלְּכֶם הַיּוֹם. לִישׁוּעָתְךָ קִוִּֽיתִי, יְיָ.

The six: Deuteronomy 33:4; a blessing-formula line; Proverbs 1:8; a two-clause
declaration ending אֵל מֶֽלֶךְ נֶאֱמָן; Shema Yisrael with Barukh Shem; Deuteronomy 4:4;
Genesis 49:18. The print gives none of them a line of its own.

[an abridged Elohai Netzor — a new indented paragraph, still under "When dressed:", with
no rubric of its own]

    אֱלֹהַי, נְצֹר לְשׁוֹנִי מֵרָע, וּשְׂפָתַי מִדַּבֵּר מִרְמָה. פְּתַח לִבִּי
    בְּתוֹרָתֶֽךָ, וּבְמִצְוֺתֶֽיךָ תִּרְדּוֹף נַפְשִׁי. יִהְיוּ לְרָצוֹן אִמְרֵי פִי
    וְהֶגְיוֹן לִבִּי לְפָנֶֽיךָ, יְיָ, צוּרִי וְגוֹאֲלִי.

Three sentences. This is **not** the text the same book prints at page 95 as the
meditation after the Shemoneh Esreh: that one has וְלִמְקַלְלַי נַפְשִׁי תִדּוֹם and
וְנַפְשִׁי כֶּעָפָר, the וְכָל הַחוֹשְׁבִים clause, the four עֲשֵׂה לְמַֽעַן clauses,
לְמַֽעַן יֵחָלְצוּן and עֹשֶׂה שָׁלוֹם, none of which are here. Where the page-95 text
punctuates מִרְמָה, and נַפְשִׁי; because the sentence runs on, this one closes both with
a full stop. Different words, so a different text: it is encoded under its own URN and
does not share the Amidah's.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

One note, on this page — the facing English page 2 carries none, and the note does not
run across the opening the way the Amidah's do. Keyed by an **unvocalized** Hebrew lemma
set in bold:

    תורה צוה לנו (Deuteronomy 33:4) is the first Hebrew verse which a father is
    directed to teach his child at a very early age (Sukkah 42a; Maimonides, Talmud
    Torah 1:6). Although the child is held to be free from religious duties, his
    father is required to make him amenable to them.

`Talmud Torah` is italic. The apparatus itself is still deferred; this is recorded here
as evidence for it.

## The transcription slice

`transcription/1.txt` is not one continuous passage the way pages 81 and 83 were. The
Hebrew Wikisource page הסידור השלם (בירנבוים)/אשכנז/שחרית לילדים assembles this page by
transclusion from FOUR foundation pages, so the slice boundaries are more of a judgement
than before. In the order the page assembles them:

    דפי יסוד/ברכות השחר וקרבנות   מודה אני; ברכת על נטילת ידיים;
                                  ברכת ציצית מילים; תורה צוה לנו
    דפי יסוד/קריאת שמע וברכותיה   אל מלך נאמן מילים; פסוק שמע ישראל;
                                  ברוך שם כבוד מלכותו לעולם ועד; לישועתך קויתי ה'
    דפי יסוד/קריאת התורה           ואתם הדבקים מילים
    דפי יסוד/תפילת העמידה          אלוהי עד מדבר מרמה; פתח ליבי עד נפשי; יהיו לרצון

The sentence punctuation between transcluded segments is the assembling page's, not the
foundation pages'. `{{נוסח}}` is resolved to the transcription's own default branch and
not to its בירנבוים branch, so that a divergence from the print is counted rather than
silently harmonised away. The transcription carries Hebrew rubric text of its own in
הוראה templates; those are dropped from the slice, because the print sets its rubrics in
English — the same departure already recorded for page 83.

## Structure notes

- Page 1 holds the whole of Shaḥarith li-Yladim on the Hebrew side; the section is one
  opening, pages 1-2, and printed page 3 begins בִּרְכוֹת הַשַּֽׁחַר.
- No conditional text anywhere on the page: nothing here depends on season, occasion,
  service or minyan.
- The two blessings differ only in their last two words, and the print sets both out in
  full rather than abbreviating the second.
- יָחֻֽלוּ carries a qubbuts, not a holam: three dots set diagonally under the ḥet, with
  no dot above it. The first draft of this reading had holam; the comparison against the
  transcription caught it and the image settled it against the reading. It is the first
  time in 275 words compared that the check has caught the reading rather than the
  transcription.
- Metegs seen: לְפָנֶֽיךָ, מֶֽלֶךְ, שֶׁהֶחֱזַֽרְתָּ, אֱמוּנָתֶֽךָ, אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ,
  קִדְּשָֽׁנוּ, וְצִוָּֽנוּ, יָדָֽיִם, לָֽנוּ, יָחֻֽלוּ, אָבִֽיךָ, אִמֶּֽךָ, קִוִּֽיתִי,
  בְּתוֹרָתֶֽךָ, וּבְמִצְוֺתֶֽיךָ.
- מִצְוֺתָיו and וּבְמִצְוֺתֶֽיךָ carry holam haser for vav, as elsewhere in this print.
