# Printed pages 28–48 (English; scan 53–73) — the korbanot to the end of the section

Eleven English pages facing Hebrew 27–47: 28, 30, 32, 34, 36, 38, 40, 42, 44, 46, 48. All
are transcribed on English Wikisource at quality 4 **from this same scan**, so the
transcription is a proofreading check and never a witness; what is listed below is what was
verified on the image.

Running head on each: page number at the outer corner, `Preliminary Morning Service` centred
in italic.

- **28** — Exodus 30:17–21 (the laver); the Yehi Ratzon; Numbers 28:1–8 (the Tamid), cut.
- **30** — the Tamid's tail; Leviticus 1:11; the Yehi Ratzon; Thou art the Lord our God;
  Exodus 30:34–36; 30:7–8; Kerithoth 6a — Pittum ha-Ketoreth, cut.
- **32** — Pittum ha-Ketoreth entire.
- **34** — its tail; the four verses; Malachi 3:4; Yoma 33a — Abaye; the first two lines of
  Ana B'khoaḥ.
- **36** — the rest of Ana B'khoaḥ; Blessed be the name; Lord of the universe; the Sabbath
  and Rosh Ḥodesh passages.
- **38** — the Rosh Ḥodesh tail; Mishnah Zebaḥim 5, mishnayoth 1–3.
- **40** — mishnayoth 4–8, cut.
- **42** — mishnah 8's tail; **Talmudic Exposition of the Scriptures**; Sifra, Introduction;
  rules 1–5 or so.
- **44** — rules 7–10 and their notes.
- **46** — rule 13; Avoth 5:23 and Malachi 3:4; **Kaddish d'Rabbanan**.
- **48** — the rest of the Kaddish, and the closing rubric.

## What the two sides do differently

These are not small. Each of them is a place where the English page and the facing Hebrew
page are **not** doing the same thing, and each has to be encoded as printed on its own side.

**The conditional rubric and its citation are one line here and two there.** The Hebrew page
35 sets `On Sabbath say:` and then `במדבר כח, ט–י` on the line below. English page 36 sets
one centred italic line: `On Sabbath say (Numbers 28:9–10):`. Same for Rosh Ḥodesh.

**A heading exists on one side only.** English page 42 carries a centred small-capitals
heading, `TALMUDIC EXPOSITION OF THE SCRIPTURES`. The facing Hebrew page 41 has **no heading
at all** — it goes from the tail of Zevaḥim straight to the citation `ספרא, פתיחה`. Checked
on both images.

**The closing rubric names a different page on each side.** Hebrew 47 ends *"On Sabbaths and
on major festivals the service is continued on page 299."* English 48 ends the same sentence
with **page 300**. Each side points at its own half of the target opening. Two aligned lines
that differ in a number, and neither is a mistake.

**The numbered sequences use different numerals.** Zevaḥim's mishnayoth are `א.` `ב.` `ג.`
in Hebrew and `1.` `2.` `3.` in English. Rabbi Ishmael's rules are `א)` in Hebrew and `1.`
in English — the Hebrew uses a parenthesis where the English uses a full stop, and the
Hebrew's two sequences are marked differently from each other while the English's are not.

**Verses are centred on one side and indented on the other.** Hebrew 33 centres
`יְיָ צְבָאוֹת עִמָּֽנוּ` and the two verses after it; English 34 sets them as indented
lines with no blank between, at the same indent as a paragraph. The same relationship the
Shema had on printed 25/26.

## The apparatus, from the English side

**A citation numeral and a commentary note can share one point in the text.** English 34's
`the fat of the shelamim,` carries superscript `3` for *Leviticus 6:5* and then a separate
commentary note keyed `שלמים`. So a position may have one of each, and the two apparatuses
are independent.

**The numerals restart at 1 on every English page**, and run to 3 at most in this range.

**The lemma is the Hebrew, and the English gloss is part of the note.** English Wikisource
renders it `{{he|שלמים|shelamim, peace-offering}}` — its own scholarly convenience. In the
print the note simply opens with the unpointed Hebrew and continues in English; there is no
glossing apparatus.

**Every commentary note's `<ref>` sits on the English page in the transcription and under
the Hebrew page in the print.** Recorded once on printed 22–26 and true here throughout:
use the transcription to learn *which* notes a page has, never *where* they sit.

## What the transcription gets wrong

Confirmed at 7–12× on the image:

1. **Page 28's note: `Ta`nith 27b`** — the print reads *Ta'anith 27b*. A backtick for an
   apostrophe and a missing letter.
2. **Page 28's note: `wll forgive`** — the print reads *will forgive*.
3. **Page 30: `It is also said;`** — the print reads `It is also said:` followed by an
   **opening quotation mark**. The transcription loses the colon and the quote, which leaves
   its quotation marks unbalanced for the rest of the paragraph.
4. **Every verse range is set with a hyphen** (`30:17-21`, `28:9-10`, `21:26-27`) where the
   print sets an **en dash**. Systematic, on every page, and worth stating once: the print's
   ranges take an en dash on both sides of the book — `שמות ל, יז–כא` and `Exodus 30:17–21`.

Items 1–3 are on pages marked proofread at quality 4, which is the lesson printed page 2 and
printed page 83 taught already.

## Two flaws in the printing itself, elsewhere in the book

Recorded here because both were found while checking this section's apparatus, and both
would otherwise look like transcription errors:

- **Printed 86's foot has a damaged glyph**: `wh.ch` for `which`, the `i` broken or
  under-inked. The English Wikisource transcription silently reads `which`, which is the
  right reading of the page.
- **Printed 84 transliterates ayin with a turned comma**: `Ta‘anith 2a`, plain at 8×. The
  same word on printed 27's foot is set the same way. The transcription uses a backtick.

## A slip of his own, on printed 34

The second numbered citation reads

    2 Psalms 46:8; 84:13: 20:10; 32:7.

with a **colon** after `84:13` where every other separator in the same note is a semicolon.
Checked at 7×: the colon is on the page. The transcription reproduces it faithfully.

Set as printed. It is recorded here so that it is not mistaken for a transcription error and
quietly corrected — the book's own inconsistencies are part of what a diplomatic edition of
it carries.

## One convention the two languages do not share

Chapter and verse take a **colon** in English (`Exodus 30:17–21`, `Psalm 141:2`) and a
**comma** in Hebrew (`שמות ל, יז–כא`). Both appear in the same book, each consistent within
its own language, and each is set as printed.
