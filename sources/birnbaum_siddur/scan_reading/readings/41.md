# Printed page 41 (Hebrew; scan 66, leaf 65)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `41`. Opens mid-mishnah and closes at the sixth of
Rabbi Ishmael's rules.

## The page

- the tail of Zevaḥim 5:8, ending `וְאֵינוֹ נֶאֱכָל אֶלָּא צָלִי`.
- `ספרא, פתיחה` — **Rabbi Ishmael's thirteen rules** begin: a lead-in,
  `רַבִּי יִשְׁמָעֵאל אוֹמֵר: בִּשְׁלֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה מִדּוֹת הַתּוֹרָה נִדְרָֽשֶׁת:`, then
  rules `א` through `ו`, one to a line.

**Only 67 words of Hebrew**, because the footnote takes the lower half of the page. The
words are in `hebrew/41.txt`.

## The rules are numbered differently from the mishnayoth

The mishnayoth on pages 37–39 are numbered `א.` with a full stop, inline with the words.
These are numbered **`א)` with a closing parenthesis**, each on its own line, the numeral
standing at the right margin and the rule indented from it. Two numbered sequences, two
different marks, four pages apart — so the numbering is per sequence and is set as printed,
never normalised to one form.

## The footnote is the largest apparatus in the unit, and it has structure

Keyed `רבי ישמעאל בן אלישע`. It fills the bottom half of this page and runs on past the
opening, and it is **not one paragraph**:

1. Prose — Rabbi Ishmael as a contemporary of Rabbi Akiba, martyred in 135; the *Baraitha
   d'Rabbi Ishmael* as the introduction to the *Sifra*, "inserted here to complete the daily
   minimum of Bible and Talmud study required of every Jew"; and a cross-reference,
   *"This section is prefaced (on page 13) by two blessings concerning Torah study."*
2. A centred sub-heading in **small capitals**: `ILLUSTRATIONS`.
3. A **numbered list**, `1.` through at least `4.`, with lettered sub-items `(a)` and `(b)`
   inside item 3 — one worked example per rule.

This matters for the apparatus more than anything else read so far. Every other note in this
unit is a paragraph or two of prose; this one carries a heading, a list, nested sub-items and
a reference to another page of the book. **A `tei:note` here has to admit block content**,
not just inline prose, and the encoding cannot assume otherwise on the strength of the
smaller notes.

Note also that the note's own citations use a **colon** between chapter and verse — `Exodus
21:2`, `Deuteronomy 24:6`, `Exodus 21:26–27` — where the Hebrew column uses a comma. Two
conventions in one book, each consistent within its own language.

## The comparison

Three differences over 67 words, all settled for the print.

**Two are qamats qatan**, recorded by `compare --settle-qamats-qatan`. **One is the combined
shin-dot/holam** in `בִּשְׁלֹשׁ`, where the edition drops the holam — the same word and the
same choice it made on printed 29's `וּשְׁלשָׁה`.
