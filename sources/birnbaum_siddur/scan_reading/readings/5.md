# Printed page 5 (Hebrew; scan 30, leaf 29)

**Running head** `ברכות השחר`, unvocalized and small, with the page number `5` beside it at
the top outer corner. This is the first page of the unit to carry one: page 3 gave its top
to the vocalized section heading and put its number at the foot instead. Both halves of
the book's rule are now visible.

Continues the tallith section begun on page 3, then opens the tefillin section.

## The page

### Rubric

    When putting on the tallith:

Roman, with `tallith` italic inside it, as on page 3.

[Blessing: להתעטף בציצית]

Defective בַּצִּיצִת again, as in every other place this page and page 3 spell it.

### Source citation, centred

    תהלים לו, ח–יא

Set small and centred above the passage, in Hebrew, and **it separates chapter from verse
with a comma** where the facing English page uses a colon. The separator is per language,
not per book, and both are his. An en dash joins the verse range.

[Psalm 36:8-11 — מה יקר חסדך]

[Yehi Ratzon after wrapping]

תַרְיַ"ג is set with gershayim. The vowel under the yod is a patach: flat, with no
descender, checked at 10x against the qamats the transcription has.

### Heading (Hebrew, centred)

    סֵֽדֶר הַנָּחַת תְּפִלִּין

The facing English page heads it PUTTING ON THE TEFILLIN. Different languages again, as
throughout this unit.

### Rubric

    Meditation before putting on the tefillin

**No terminal colon.** Every other rubric read so far ends in one; this one does not. His
wording is set as printed and is never normalised, so the colon is not supplied.

[Hineni Mekhaven — the meditation]

The paragraph **does not finish on this page**. It breaks after בְּהוֹצִיאוֹ אוֹתָֽנוּ and
continues on page 7, so the page turn falls mid-sentence and `tei:pb` belongs inside the
`tei:p`.

Note the semicolon after בָּעוֹלָם, checked at 9x. Birnbaum uses both marks and they are
indistinguishable at band magnification.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

One long commentary note on the tefillin, keyed by the catchword תפלין, describing how the
shel rosh and shel yad are made and what the shins and the knots spell.

**It does not finish on this page either.** It breaks mid-sentence and continues at the
foot of the facing English page 6 — the second running note in three pages, so the
arrangement page 3 introduced is the normal case in this unit rather than an exception.

## Three readings corrected before committing

All three were pointing, all three were caught by the diff against the transcription, and
all three were settled against the reading at 14x:

- `בַּהֲנָחַת` read where the print sets **בְּהַנָּחַת** — shva under the bet, patach
  under the he, dagesh in the nun.
- `לְהָנִֽיחַ` read where the print sets **לְהַנִּֽיחַ**.
- `יְבִאֲךָ` read defective where the print sets it plene, **יְבִיאֲךָ**.

The last two are places the Wikisource text carries a `{{נוסח}}` template naming
Birnbaum's own spelling against other editions. The template was right both times. See
`specs/birnbaum_scan/accuracy.md`, which revises what the first three pages concluded
about how far bands can be trusted for pointing.

One difference was briefly left unresolved — whether the dot on the second שׂ/שׁ of שעשה
sits left or right — on the ground that the crop could not place it. That was the wrong
call: עָשָׂה takes a sin, so the print's reading was never in doubt, and the
transcription's shin is its own error. Recorded as `print`.

The mistake is worth naming, because it will recur: reaching for "the image cannot settle
it" when what is actually missing is knowledge of the word. From inside the crop the two
look the same.
