# Printed page 25 (Hebrew; scan 50, leaf 49)

Running head `ברכות השחר`, page number `25` at the top outer corner. Opens mid-sentence,
finishing the `אֲבָל אֲנַֽחְנוּ עַמְּךָ` begun on page 23, and closes at the end of a
paragraph.

## The page

The tail of `אֲבָל אֲנַֽחְנוּ עַמְּךָ`, ending in the colon that introduces the Shema; the
Shema verse and `בָּרוּךְ שֵׁם`, each on its own centred line; `אַתָּה הוּא עַד שֶׁלֹּא
נִבְרָא הָעוֹלָם` with its closing blessing; and `אַתָּה הוּא, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ,
בַּשָּׁמַֽיִם וּבָאָֽרֶץ`.

No heading and no rubric. The words are in `hebrew/25.txt`.

## Two speaker labels, both inline

`Reader` in small roman capitals, twice, and on this page **inline** rather than in the
margin — set within the line, immediately before the words it governs, so reading order
puts it first:

- before `אַשְׁרֵֽינוּ, שֶׁאֲנַֽחְנוּ מַשְׁכִּימִים`, where the reader takes up the lead-in
  to the Shema;
- before `וּבִישׁוּעָתְךָ תָּרוּם`, the close of `אַתָּה הוּא`.

Page 19's label stood in the left margin; these do not. Same vocabulary, two placements,
and the placement is worth checking on every page rather than assumed from the last one —
crop the line before deciding, because a label at the left edge of a full line and a label
in the margin look alike at a glance.

## The Shema and Barukh Shem, and where they are realised

Both verses are set centred, each as its own line with space above and below. They are
already realised in Shaḥarith li-Yladim (printed page 1), which is **not** the canonical
place for them — that unit should transclude, not be transcluded from. This page is the
one that raises the question; it does not settle it. Realise them from here as every other
page is realised, and consolidate afterwards.

## Footnote apparatus (bottom of the page, below a rule)

One note, keyed to the span `קדש את שמך–ברבים` — lemma given unpointed, with an **en dash**
between the two ends of the span, which is how he writes a lemma that covers a passage
rather than a word:

> God manifests his saving power to the entire world by delivering those who suffer
> martyrdom for his sake (Ezekiel 36:23; 39:7).

It does not run across the opening; it begins and ends under this page.

## The comparison

Eleven differences over 181 words, all settled for the print, none needing a question.

**Four are qamats qatan**, settled by rule — the print sets one glyph and the edition
interprets it.

**Three are metegs** the edition drops: `שֶׁאֲנַֽחְנוּ`, `אָבִֽינוּ`, and `אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ` in
the Shema verse. Each checked on the image at 12×; each is a distinct vertical stroke
beside the vowel, not part of a qamats's own tick.

**One is a hataf segol the edition sets as a plain segol** — it has `אֶלֹהֵֽינוּ` before
`מַה שֶּׁכָּתוּב`, where the print has `אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ`. Five marks under the alef on the
image, not three. An edition typo, not a variant.

**Three are the parting of the Shema and Barukh Shem** into separate centred paragraphs,
which the print makes and the edition's markup sets as two lines of one centred block.

One correction to the reading came out of this page: `כָל בָּאֵי עוֹלָם` was read with a
dagesh it does not have. The כ beside `בָּאֵי` on the same line settles it — that ב carries
a plain visible dot and this כ carries none.
