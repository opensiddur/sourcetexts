# Printed 768 — IA n792

## harachaman_livelihood

May the Merciful One grant us a respectable livelihood.

## harachaman_yoke

May the Merciful One break the yoke from our neck; may he lead us securely into our land.

## harachaman_house

May the Merciful One send abundant blessings into this house and upon this table at which we have eaten.

## harachaman_elijah

May the Merciful One send us Elijah the prophet—of blessed memory—to bring us the good tidings of deliverance and comfort.

## harachaman_self

May the Merciful One bless me, (my wife, my children) and all that is mine.

## harachaman_hosts

May the Merciful One bless (my dear father) the master of this house and (my dear mother) the mistress of this house, their entire family and all that is theirs.

## harachaman_all

May he bless us and all that is ours; may he bless us all alike with a perfect blessing even as our forefathers Abraham, Isaac and Jacob were blessed in every way; and let us say, Amen.

## bamarom

May they in heaven plead for all of us that we may have enduring peace. May we receive gifts from the Lord, justice from our saving God; may we be in the good graces of God and men.

## harachaman_shabbat

May the Merciful One grant us the day which will be all Sabbath and rest in life everlasting.

## harachaman_rc

May the Merciful One renew for us this month for happiness and blessedness.

## commentary — bamarom

במרום expresses the idea found in the Talmud (Ḥullin 92b) that the angels on high plead the cause of Israel. עליהם refers to the hosts who entertain the guests. ונשא ברכה and ונמצא חן are somewhat modified quotations from Psalm 24:5 and Proverbs 3:4.

## commentary — harachaman_shabbat

יום שכולו שבת... is borrowed from the Mishnah (Tamid, end).
