# Weekday Torah reading — printed 119–128 (IA n143–n152)

Read from the scan. The requested Hebrew range is n143–n151; n152 supplies the facing English ending. Stop before Ashrei in both languages. Page-turn markers and the parenthesized second לעלא below are diplomatic readings, not renderer punctuation.

## Printed rubrics and sequence

The Torah is read on Mondays and Thursdays, Rosh Ḥodesh, Ḥol ha-Mo‘ed, Ḥanukkah, Purim, and on fast days.

The ark is opened.
Reader and Congregation: Vayehi binsoa; Barukh shenatan.
Zohar, Wayyakhel: Berikh shemeh (continued on 121/122; “Reader” before ותשלים on the Hebrew page only).

The Reader takes the Torah and says: Gadelu.
Congregation: Lekha; Romemu; Av haraḥamim.

The Torah is placed on the desk. The Reader unrolls it and says: Vetiggaleh through Ha-el tamim.
Congregation and Reader: Ve-attem.

The person called to the Torah recites: Barekhu.
Congregation responds: Barukh.
He repeats the response and continues: Asher baḥar.
The Torah is read. Then he recites: Asher natan.

בִּרְכַת הַגּוֹמֵל / THANKSGIVING
One who has come safely through a dangerous experience recites:
Congregation responds: Mi shegemalekha.

When the reading of the Torah is concluded, the Reader recites: Half Kaddish.

When the Torah is raised, the Congregation recites: Vezot ha-Torah; Etz ḥayyim.

On Mondays and Thursdays (if Taḥanun has been said), before returning the Torah to the ark, the Reader recites:

The Reader takes the Torah and says: Yehalelu.
Congregation: Hodo; Vayarem.
תהלים כד / Psalm 24.

While the Torah is being placed in the ark: Uvnuḥo through Hashivenu.
The ark is closed. The morning service continues.

## Hebrew and English passages

### vayehi_binsoa — Hebrew 119–119; English 120–120

וַיְהִי בִּנְסֹֽעַ הָאָרֹן וַיֹּֽאמֶר מֹשֶׁה: קוּמָה יְיָ, וְיָפֻֽצוּ אֹיְבֶֽיךָ, וְיָנֻֽסוּ מְשַׂנְאֶֽיךָ מִפָּנֶֽיךָ.

And it came to pass, whenever the ark started, Moses would say: “Arise, O Lord, and let thy enemies be scattered; let those who hate thee flee before thee.”

כִּי מִצִּיּוֹן תֵּצֵא תוֹרָה, וּדְבַר יְיָ מִירוּשָׁלָֽיִם.

Truly, out of Zion shall come forth Torah, and the word of the Lord out of Jerusalem.

בָּרוּךְ שֶׁנָּתַן תּוֹרָה לְעַמּוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל בִּקְדֻשָּׁתוֹ.

Blessed be he who in his holiness gave the Torah to his people Israel.

### berikh_shemeh — Hebrew 119–121; English 120–122

זוהר, ויקהל / Zohar, Wayyakhel

בְּרִיךְ שְׁמֵהּ דְּמָרֵא עָלְמָא, בְּרִיךְ כִּתְרָךְ וְאַתְרָךְ. יְהֵא רְעוּתָךְ עִם עַמָּךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל לְעָלַם, וּפֻרְקַן יְמִינָךְ אַחֲזֵי לְעַמָּךְ בְּבֵית מַקְדְּשָׁךְ: וּלְאַמְטוּיֵי לָֽנָא מִטּוּב נְהוֹרָךְ, וּלְקַבֵּל צְלוֹתָֽנָא בְּרַחֲמִין. יְהֵא רַעֲוָא קֳדָמָךְ, דְּתוֹרִיךְ לָן חַיִּין בְּטִיבוּתָא.

Blessed be the name of the Lord of the universe! Blessed be thy crown and thy dominion. May thy good will ever abide with thy people Israel. Reveal thy saving power to thy people in thy sanctuary; bestow on us the good gift of thy light, and accept our prayer in mercy. May it be thy will to prolong our life in happiness.

וְלֶהֱוֵא אֲנָא פְקִידָא בְּגוֹ צַדִּיקַיָּא, לְמִרְחַם עֲלַי וּלְמִנְטַר יָתִי וְיָת כָּל דִּי לִי וְדִי לְעַמָּךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל. אַנְתְּ הוּא זָן לְכֹֽלָּא וּמְפַרְנֵס לְכֹֽלָּא. אַנְתְּ הוּא שַׁלִּיט עַל כֹּֽלָּא: אַנְתְּ הוּא דְּשַׁלִּיט עַל מַלְכַיָּא, וּמַלְכוּתָא דִילָךְ הִיא. אֲנָא עַבְדָּא דְקֻדְשָׁא בְּרִיךְ הוּא, דְּסָגִֽידְנָא קַמֵּהּ וּמִקַּמֵּי דִּיקַר אוֹרַיְתֵהּ בְּכָל עִדָּן וְעִדָּן. לָא עַל אֱנָשׁ רָחִֽיצְנָא, וְלָא עַל בַּר אֱלָהִין סָמִֽיכְנָא, אֶלָּא בֵּאלָהָא דִשְׁמַיָּא, דְּהוּא אֱלָהָא קְשׁוֹט, וְאוֹרַיְתֵהּ קְשׁוֹט, וּנְבִיאֽוֹהִי קְשׁוֹט, וּמַסְגֵּא לְמֶעְבַּד טָבְוָן וּקְשׁוֹט. בֵּהּ אֲנָא רָחִיץ, וְלִשְׁמֵהּ קַדִּישָׁא יַקִּירָא אֲנָא אֵמַר תֻּשְׁבְּחָן. יְהֵא רַעֲוָא קֳדָמָךְ, דְּתִפְתַּח לִבָּאִי {pb:121}בְּאוֹרַיְתָא, [Reader] וְתַשְׁלִים מִשְׁאֲלִין דְּלִבָּאִי וְלִבָּא דְכָל עַמָּךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל לְטַב וּלְחַיִּין וְלִשְׁלָם.

Let me also be counted among the righteous, so that thou mayest have compassion on me and shelter me and mine and all that belong to thy people Israel. Thou art he who nourishes and sustains all; thou art he who rules over all; thou art he who rules over kings, for dominion is thine. I am the servant of the Holy One, blessed be he, before whom and before whose glorious Torah I bow at all times. Not in man do I put my trust, nor do I rely on any angel, but only in the God of heaven who is the God of truth, whose Torah is truth and whose Prophets are truth, and who performs many deeds of goodness and truth. In him I put my trust, and to his holy and glorious name I utter praises. May it be thy will to {pb:122}open my heart to thy Torah, and to fulfill the wishes of my heart and of the heart of all thy people Israel for happiness, life and peace.

### gadelu — Hebrew 121–121; English 122–122

גַּדְּלוּ לַיְיָ אִתִּי, וּנְרוֹמְמָה שְׁמוֹ יַחְדָּו.

Exalt the Lord with me, and let us extol his name together.

### lekha_adonai — Hebrew 121–121; English 122–122

לְךָ יְיָ הַגְּדֻלָּה וְהַגְּבוּרָה, וְהַתִּפְאֶֽרֶת וְהַנֵּֽצַח וְהַהוֹד, כִּי כֹל בַּשָּׁמַֽיִם וּבָאָֽרֶץ. לְךָ יְיָ הַמַּמְלָכָה וְהַמִּתְנַשֵּׂא לְכֹל לְרֹאשׁ.

Thine, O Lord, is the greatness and the power, the glory and the victory and the majesty; for all that is in heaven and on earth is thine; thine, O Lord, is the kingdom, and thou art supreme over all.

רוֹמְמוּ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, וְהִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לַהֲדֹם רַגְלָיו, קָדוֹשׁ הוּא.

Exalt the Lord our God, and worship at his footstool; holy is he.

רוֹמְמוּ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, וְהִשְׁתַּחֲווּ לְהַר קָדְשׁוֹ, כִּי קָדוֹשׁ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ.

Exalt the Lord our God, and worship at his holy mountain, for holy is the Lord our God.

### av_harachamim — Hebrew 121–121; English 122–122

אַב הָרַחֲמִים, הוּא יְרַחֵם עַם עֲמוּסִים, וְיִזְכֹּר בְּרִית אֵיתָנִים, וְיַצִּיל נַפְשׁוֹתֵֽינוּ מִן הַשָּׁעוֹת הָרָעוֹת, וְיִגְעַר בְּיֵֽצֶר הָרָע מִן הַנְּשׂוּאִים, וְיָחֹן אוֹתָֽנוּ לִפְלֵטַת עוֹלָמִים, וִימַלֵּא מִשְׁאֲלוֹתֵֽינוּ בְּמִדָּה טוֹבָה, יְשׁוּעָה וְרַחֲמִים.

May the merciful Father have compassion on the people who have been upheld by him, and remember the covenant with the patriarchs; may he deliver us from evil times, and check the evil impulse in those who have been tended by him; may he graciously grant us everlasting deliverance, and in his goodness fulfill our petitions for salvation and mercy.

### vetiggaleh — Hebrew 121–121; English 122–122

וְתִגָּלֶה וְתֵרָאֶה מַלְכוּתוֹ עָלֵֽינוּ בִּזְמַן קָרוֹב, וְיָחֹן פְּלֵטָתֵֽנוּ וּפְלֵטַת עַמּוֹ בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל לְחֵן וּלְחֶֽסֶד, לְרַחֲמִים וּלְרָצוֹן, וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן. הַכֹּל הָבוּ גֹֽדֶל לֵאלֹהֵֽינוּ, וּתְנוּ כָבוֹד לַתּוֹרָה. כֹּהֵן קְרָב; יַעֲמֹד (פְּלוֹנִי בֶּן פְּלוֹנִי) הַכֹּהֵן.

May his kingdom soon be revealed and made visible to us; may he be gracious to our remnant, the remnant of his people, the house of Israel, granting them grace and kindness, mercy and favor; and let us say, Amen. Let us all ascribe greatness to our God, and give honor to the Torah. Let the Kohen come forward [the Reader names the first person called to the Torah].

בָּרוּךְ שֶׁנָּתַן תּוֹרָה לְעַמּוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל בִּקְדֻשָּׁתוֹ.

Blessed be he who in his holiness gave the Torah to his people Israel.

תּוֹרַת יְיָ תְּמִימָה, מְשִֽׁיבַת נָֽפֶשׁ. עֵדוּת יְיָ נֶאֱמָנָה, מַחְכִּֽימַת פֶּֽתִי.

The Lord’s Torah is perfect, refreshing the soul; the Lord’s testimony is trustworthy, teaching the simple man wisdom.

פִּקּוּדֵי יְיָ יְשָׁרִים, מְשַׂמְּחֵי לֵב: מִצְוַת יְיָ בָּרָה, מְאִירַת עֵינָֽיִם.

The Lord’s precepts are right, gladdening the heart; the Lord’s commandment is clear, enlightening the eyes.

יְיָ עֹז לְעַמּוֹ יִתֵּן: יְיָ יְבָרֵךְ אֶת עַמּוֹ בַשָּׁלוֹם.

The Lord will give strength to his people; the Lord will bless his people with peace.

הָאֵל תָּמִים דַּרְכּוֹ; אִמְרַת יְיָ צְרוּפָה, מָגֵן הוּא לְכֹל הַחֹסִים בּוֹ.

The way of God is perfect; the word of the Lord is pure; he is a shield to all who trust in him.

### veatem — Hebrew 121–121; English 122–122

וְאַתֶּם הַדְּבֵקִים בַּיְיָ אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, חַיִּים כֻּלְּכֶם הַיּוֹם.

And you who cling to the Lord your God are all alive today.

### barekhu — Hebrew 123–123; English 124–124

בָּרְכוּ אֶת יְיָ הַמְּבֹרָךְ.

Bless the Lord who is blessed.

בָּרוּךְ יְיָ הַמְּבֹרָךְ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד.

Blessed be the Lord who is blessed forever and ever.

### asher_bachar — Hebrew 123–123; English 124–124

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, מֶֽלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר בָּֽחַר בָּֽנוּ מִכָּל הָעַמִּים וְנָֽתַן לָֽנוּ אֶת תּוֹרָתוֹ. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ, נוֹתֵן הַתּוֹרָה.

Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who hast chosen us from all peoples, and hast given us thy Torah. Blessed art thou, O Lord, Giver of the Torah.

### asher_natan — Hebrew 123–123; English 124–124

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, מֶֽלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר נָֽתַן לָֽנוּ תּוֹרַת אֱמֶת וְחַיֵּי עוֹלָם נָטַע בְּתוֹכֵֽנוּ. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ, נוֹתֵן הַתּוֹרָה.

Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who hast given us the Torah of truth, and hast planted everlasting life in our midst. Blessed art thou, O Lord, Giver of the Torah.

### hagomel — Hebrew 123–123; English 124–124

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה, יְיָ אֱלֹהֵֽינוּ, מֶֽלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, הַגּוֹמֵל לְחַיָּבִים טוֹבוֹת, שֶׁגְּמָלַֽנִי כָּל טוֹב.

Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who bestowest favors on the undeserving, and hast shown me every kindness.

מִי שֶׁגְּמָלְךָ כָּל טוֹב, הוּא יִגְמָלְךָ כָּל טוֹב סֶֽלָה.

May he who has shown you every kindness ever deal kindly with you.

### vezot_hatorah — Hebrew 125–125; English 126–126

וְזֹאת הַתּוֹרָה אֲשֶׁר שָׂם מֹשֶׁה לִפְנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.

This is the Torah which Moses placed before the children of Israel.

עַל פִּי יְיָ בְּיַד מֹשֶׁה.

It is in accordance with the Lord’s command through Moses.

עֵץ חַיִּים הִיא לַמַּחֲזִיקִים בָּהּ, וְתֹמְכֶֽיהָ מְאֻשָּׁר.

It is a tree of life to those who take hold of it, and happy are those who support it.

דְּרָכֶֽיהָ דַרְכֵי נֹֽעַם, וְכָל נְתִיבוֹתֶֽיהָ שָׁלוֹם.

Its ways are pleasant ways, and all its paths are peace.

אֹֽרֶךְ יָמִים בִּימִינָהּ; בִּשְׂמֹאלָהּ עֹֽשֶׁר וְכָבוֹד.

Long life is in its right hand, and in its left hand are riches and honor.

יְיָ חָפֵץ לְמַֽעַן צִדְקוֹ, יַגְדִּיל תּוֹרָה וְיַאְדִּיר.

The Lord was pleased, because of his righteousness, to render the Torah great and glorious.

### yehi_ratzon — Hebrew 125–125; English 126–126

יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלִּפְנֵי אָבִֽינוּ שֶׁבַּשָּׁמַֽיִם לְכוֹנֵן אֶת בֵּית חַיֵּֽינוּ, וּלְהָשִׁיב אֶת שְׁכִינָתוֹ בְּתוֹכֵֽנוּ בִּמְהֵרָה בְיָמֵֽינוּ, וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.

May it be the will of our Father who is in heaven to establish our Temple, and to restore his divine presence in our midst, speedily in our days; and let us say, Amen.

יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלִּפְנֵי אָבִֽינוּ שֶׁבַּשָּׁמַֽיִם לְרַחֵם עָלֵֽינוּ וְעַל פְּלֵטָתֵֽנוּ, וְלִמְנֹֽעַ מַשְׁחִית וּמַגֵּפָה מֵעָלֵֽינוּ וּמֵעַל כָּל עַמּוֹ בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.

May it be the will of our Father who is in heaven to have mercy on us and on our remnant, and to ward off destruction and pestilence from us and from all his people, the house of Israel; and let us say, Amen.

יְהִי רָצוֹן מִלִּפְנֵי אָבִֽינוּ שֶׁבַּשָּׁמַֽיִם לְקַיֵּם בָּֽנוּ חַכְמֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, הֵם וּנְשֵׁיהֶם וּבְנֵיהֶם וּבְנוֹתֵיהֶם, וְתַלְמִידֵיהֶם וְתַלְמִידֵי תַלְמִידֵיהֶם, בְּכָל מְקוֹמוֹת מוֹשְׁבוֹתֵיהֶם, וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.

May it be the will of our Father who is in heaven to preserve everywhere among us the wise men of Israel and their wives, their sons and daughters, their disciples and the disciples of their disciples; and let us say, Amen.

## Printed commentary and numbered citations

The Berikh shemeh commentary starts on 119 and continues on 120. The Hagomel commentary starts on 123 and continues on 124. Footnote 3 on 128 belongs to Ashrei and is outside this slice.

קריאת התורה on Mondays and Thursdays was instituted by Ezra, according to tradition, in order not to let three days go by without the instruction of the Torah. Originally, the persons called to the Torah read the passages apportioned to them. This custom was abandoned in order not to embarrass those who lacked proper training.

בריך שמיה is taken from the Zohar, the fundamental book of Kabbalah, which was first made known in the thirteenth century and ascribed to Rabbi Simeon ben Yoḥai of the second century. The Zohar introduces this inspiring and uplifting prayer as follows: “When the Torah is taken out to be read before the congregation, the heavenly gates of mercy are opened and the divine love is aroused; therefore one should recite: בריך הוא . . .” The term בר אלהין (“angel”) is found in Daniel 3:25.

ברכת הגומל , known as “gomel benshen,” is derived from Berakhoth 54b, where it is said that four classes of men should offer thanks: 1) those who have made a voyage by sea, 2) or a journey through the desert, 3) or have recovered from a severe illness, 4) or have been released from prison.
