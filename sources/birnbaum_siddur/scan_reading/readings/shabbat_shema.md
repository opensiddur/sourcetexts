### Festival Ha-me’ir

Hebrew: shared weekday Ha-me’ir, including Psalm 104:24.

In mercy thou givest light to the earth and to those who dwell on it; in thy goodness thou renewest the work of creation every day, constantly. How great are thy works, O Lord! God is the Lord of all creation;|Blessed and praised is he by every soul.|His greatness and goodness fill the universe;|Knowledge and wisdom surround him.

### El Adon, stanza 2

He is exalted above the celestial beings,|And adorned in glory above the chariot.|Purity and justice stand before his throne;|Kindness and mercy are in his glorious presence.

Good are the luminaries which our God has created;|He made them with knowledge, wisdom and insight;|He placed in them energy and power|To have dominion over the world.

### La’el Asher Shavat

לָאֵל אֲשֶׁר שָׁבַת מִכָּל הַמַּעֲשִׂים בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי; הִתְעַלָּה וְיָשַׁב עַל כִּסֵּא כְבוֹדוֹ; תִּפְאֶרֶת עָטָה לְיוֹם הַמְּנוּחָה, עֹנֶג קָרָא לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת. זֶה שֶׁבַח שֶׁל יוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי, שֶׁבּוֹ שָׁבַת אֵל מִכָּל {pb:341}מְלַאכְתּוֹ. וְיוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי מְשַׁבֵּחַ וְאוֹמֵר: מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, טוֹב לְהוֹדוֹת לַייָ. לְפִיכָךְ יְפָאֲרוּ וִיבָרְכוּ לָאֵל כָּל יְצוּרָיו; שֶׁבַח, יְקָר וּגְדֻלָּה יִתְּנוּ לָאֵל מֶלֶךְ, יוֹצֵר כֹּל, הַמַּנְחִיל מְנוּחָה לְעַמּוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל בִּקְדֻשָּׁתוֹ בְּיוֹם שַׁבַּת קֹדֶשׁ. שִׁמְךָ יְיָ אֱלֹהֵינוּ יִתְקַדָּשׁ, וְזִכְרְךָ מַלְכֵּנוּ יִתְפָּאַר, בַּשָּׁמַיִם מִמַּעַל וְעַל הָאָרֶץ מִתָּחַת. תִּתְבָּרַךְ, מוֹשִׁיעֵנוּ, עַל שֶׁבַח מַעֲשֵׂה יָדֶיךָ, וְעַל מְאוֹרֵי אוֹר שֶׁעָשִׂיתָ; יְפָאֲרוּךָ סֶּלָה.

To God who rested from all the work of creation on the seventh day, and ascended to sit upon his throne of glory. He vested the day of rest with beauty, and called the Sabbath a delight. Such is the distinction of the seventh day, on which God rested from {pb:342}all his work. The seventh day itself utters praise, saying: “A song of the Sabbath day—It is good to give thanks to the Lord.” Therefore, let all God’s creatures glorify and bless him; let them attribute excellence, glory and grandeur to God, the King and Creator of all, who in his holiness bestows rest upon his people Israel on the holy Sabbath day. Thy name, Lord our God, shall be hallowed; thy fame, our King, shall be glorified in heaven above and on earth beneath. Be thou blessed, our Deliverer, for thy excellent handiwork, and for the bright luminaries which thou hast made; they ever render thee glory.

## New commentary

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/shacharit/shema/yotzer_or/festival/el_barukh

אל ברוך גדול דעה is an alphabetic acrostic ending with תמיד.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:poem:el_adon

אל אדון is an alphabetical hymn, generally attributed to the Yorde Merkavah, mystics of the eighth century, who applied their minds to theosophy. The Tur mentions a variant reading, והקטין instead of והתקין, according to which the clause concerning the moon refers to the talmudic tradition that God diminished the original size of the moon (Ḥullin 60b). El Adon is a praise of God who created the seven seemingly “wandering” celestial bodies (כוכבי לכת). Having spoken of the sun and the moon, the poet alludes to the five planets Saturn (שבתאי), Venus (נוגה), Mercury (כוכב), Jupiter (צדק), and Mars (מאדים), by means of the initials of the words שבח נותנים כל צבא מרום.

### urn:x-opensiddur:text:prayer:yotzer_or/lael_asher_shavat

לאל אשר שבת is found in the geonic liturgy. Like the other Sabbath additions to the Yotser benediction, it probably belongs to the talmudic period. According to the Midrash, Adam and the Sabbath sang in unison: “It is good to give thanks to the Lord”; hence ויום השביעי משבח ואומר.

## Encoding and collation decisions

- `urn:x-opensiddur:text:siddur:shabbat/shacharit/shema` addresses this complete section. It extends the existing Sabbath/festival Shacharit service after Pesukei Dezimrah, with child bookmarks for blessings before Shema, Shema, and the blessing after Shema. The service does not force a calendar date.
- The festival branch requires a festival and not Sabbath. The Sabbath branch therefore wins when the festival falls on Sabbath. Unknown calendar settings leave the alternatives visible with their instructions; redundant printed parentheses are omitted.
- El Adon has a poem URN and six stanza milestones. Hakol Yodukha, Ein Kearkekha and La’el Asher Shavat use prayer namespace addresses. The abbreviated Sabbath Ha-me’ir and English variant occurrences have siddur namespace addresses. Correspondence boundaries are independent of printed paragraphs.
- For the local English variants, the importer reuses the established Hebrew readings, with source attributes and local correspondence boundaries so each variant aligns with the appropriate paragraph. Other common passages remain transclusions.
- La’el Asher Shavat’s Psalm 92 quotation carries biblical sources; it quotes all of verse 1 and part of verse 2, whose complete canonical text is already encoded in Psalm 92.
- Enlargements confirm הַמְּשֻׁבָּח and וְהַמְּפֹאָר with dagesh in mem, כְּעֶרְכְּךָ with sheva, full סוֹבְבִים, לָאֵל מֶלֶךְ with qamats, יִתְקַדָּשׁ with qamats, and the forms כַּייָ / לַייָ without an added sheva under the first yod. The commentary spells Venus נוגה and quotes שבח נותנים כל צבא מרום without לו.
