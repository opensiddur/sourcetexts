The  Shema,  Israel’s  confession  of  faith,  expresses  the  duty  of  loving  and serving  God  with  our  whole  being.  The  second  paragraph  demands  that  wo give  living  expression  to  our  love  of  God  by  careful  observance  of  his  pre- cepts which  are  designed  to  assure  our  happiness.  The  third  section  contains the  law  of  tsitsith,  intended  to  remind  us  constantly  of  our  duties  towards God,  and  a   warning  against  following  the  evil  impulses  of  the  heart.  The Shema,  sounding  the  keynote  of  Judaism,  is  the  oldest  prayer  of  the  Siddur. In  the  morning  service  the  Shema  is  preceded  by  two  blessings  and  followed by  one;  in  the  evening  service  it  is  preceded  by  two  blessings  and  followed  by two.  This  is  in  keeping  with  the  expression:  “Seven  times  a   day  I   praise  thee’’ (Psalm  119:164;  Berakhoth  lib).
