Hence,  the  Hasmoneans  and  all  the  Jews  alike  instituted these  eight  days  as  a   time  of  feasting  and  rejoicing,  like  any  festival prescribed  in  the  Torah,  and  of  kindling  lights  to  commemorate  the victories  God  had  given  them.  Mourning  and  fasting  are  forbidden on  Hanukkah,  except  in  the  case  of  an  individual’s  vow  which  must be  discharged.  Nevertheless,  the  Hasmoneans  did  not  prohibit work  on  this  holiday.
From  that  time  on  the  Greek  government  was  stripped  of  its renown.  The  Hasmoneans  and  their  descendants  ruled  for  two hundred  and  six  years,  until  the  destruction  of  the  Temple.
