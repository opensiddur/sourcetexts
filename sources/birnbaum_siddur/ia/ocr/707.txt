"pVwr,  the  custom  of  symbolically  casting  the  sins  into  a   running  stream, presumably  dates  from  the  fourteenth  century.  It  is  mentioned  for  the  first time  in  Refer  Maharil  by  Rabbi  Jacob  Moelln  (1355-1427),  the  leading  Jewish authority  of  his  time.  He  explains  it  as  a   reminder  of  the  Akedah,  concerning which  the  Midrash  relates  that  Satan,  in  an  effort  to  prevent  Abraham  from sacrificing  Isaac,  transformed  himself  into  a   deep  stream  on  the  road  leading  to Mount  Moriah.  Plunging  into  the  stream,  Abraham  and  Isaac  prayed  for divine  aid,  whereupon  the  place  became  dry  land  again.  The  name (“thou  wilt  cast”)  is  derived  from  Micah  7:19.
