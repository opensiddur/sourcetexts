The  formal  betrothal  was  performed  in  the house  of  the  bride  months  before  the  actual  marriage  took  place  in  the  home  of the  groom.  Hence,  two  cups  of  wine  are  used  in  modern  wedding  ceremonies, one  for  erusin  and  one  for  nissuin.  The  two  celebrations  are  now  separated only  by  the  reading  of  the  Aramaic  Kethubah,  the  marriage  contract  specifying the  mutual  obligations  between  husband  and  wife  (Tosafoth,  Kethuboth  7b). Since  the  fourteenth  century  it  has  been  customary  to  have  a   rabbi  perform  the ceremony  under  a   huppah,  representing  symbolically  the  future  home  of  the couple.
