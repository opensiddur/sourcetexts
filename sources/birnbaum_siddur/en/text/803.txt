<noinclude><pagequality level="4" user="Kathleen.wright5" />{{rh|778|''Nght Prayer''|}}</noinclude>{{c|''On seeing a person of abnormal appearance:''}}
Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who dost vary the aspect of thy creatures.

{{c|''On seeing a person of profound Torah wisdom:''}}
Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who hast imparted of thy wisdom to those who revere thee.

{{c|''On seeing a person of profound secular learning:''}}
Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who hast imparted of thy wisdom to flesh and blood.

{{c|''On hearing bad tidings:''}}
Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, the true Judge.

{{c|''On hearing good tidings:''}}
Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who art good and beneficent.


{{c|NIGHT PRAYER<ref>{{he|המפיל|The following blessing, which is called by the first word after its introductory formula, "Ha-Mapil".}} is an abbreviation from a passage cited in Berakhoth 60b. The prayer recited before going to bed is called קריאת שמע because the ''Shema'' forms its chief element. According to the Talmud, one ought to read the ''Shema'' before retiring at night in addition to its recital in the evening service (Berakhith 4b).</ref>}}
{{c|''Recited before retiring''}}

Blessed art thou, Lord our God, King of the universe, who closest my eyes in sleep, my eyelids in slumber.<ref>{{he|המפיל חבלי שינה...}} literally means “who let down the cords, or chains, of sleep upon my eyes.” The reference is to the whole body, metaphorically represented as chained in sleep.</ref> May it be thy will, Lord my God and God of my fathers, to grant that I lie down in peace and that I rise up in peace. Let not my thoughts upset me–nor evil dreams, nor sinful fancies. May my family<ref>{{he|מטתי|literally "my bed", but translated "family"}} is here used figuratively in the sense of my offspring; compare the expressions {{he|שהיתה מטתו שלימה}} and {{he|שמא חס ושלום יש במטתי פסוק}} (Pesaḥim 56a).</ref> ever be perfect in thy sight. Grant me light, lest I sleep the sleep of death; for it is thou who givest light to the eyes.<ref>{{he|אישון}} and {{he|בת עין}} are synonyms denoting the apple of the eye.</ref> Blessed art thou, O Lord, whose majesty gives light to the whole world.<noinclude>{{rule}}
