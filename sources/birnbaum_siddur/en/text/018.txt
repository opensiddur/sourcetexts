Similarly, in the Hymn of Glory “the mystic utterance of thy servants” should be corrected to ''amidst thy servants''.

The famous poem ''Adon Olam'' celebrates the eternity of God, and yet the initial phrase {{he|אֲדוֹן עוֹלָם}} is invariably translated “Lord of the universe” instead of ''Eternal Lord''. The terms {{he|חַגִּים}} and {{he|זְמַנִּים}} are frequently used synonymously in the sense of ''festivals'', and yet {{he|זְמַן חֵרוּתֵֽנוּ}} is generally rendered “the Season of our Freedom” instead of ''our Festival of Freedom''. {{he|גֵּרֵי הַצֶּֽדֶק}} means ''the true proselytes'', that is, those who have accepted Judaism out of inner conviction; it does not mean “strangers of righteousness” or “proselytes of righteousness.” {{he|הַמְּיַחֲדִים בָּתֵּי כְנֵסִיּוֹת}} signifies ''who dedicate synagogues'', and not “who unite to form synagogues.”<ref>Singer, ''Daily Prayer Book'', page 152.</ref>

The oft-repeated “Blessed be the name of his glorious kingdom” is incorrectly translated. Equally incorrect is “Blessed be his name, whose glorious kingdom” or “Blessed be his glorious kingdom.” ''His glorious Majesty''—God himself—is here the object of praise, and not his kingdom. The response {{he|בָּרוּךְ שֵׁם כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתוֹ}}, which was used in the Temple in place of Amen,<ref>Ta‘anith 16b.</ref> is the equivalent of the ''Kaddish'' response {{he|יְהֵא שְׁמֵהּ רַבָּא מְבָרַךְ}} (“may his great name be blessed”).<ref>Compare Daniel 2:20. Targum Yerushalmi (Deuteronomy 6:4) interchanges the two formulas.</ref> {{he|שֵׁם כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתוֹ}} connotes ''His Majesty the King'', a circumlocution for the name of God and similar to {{he|שֵׁם כְּבוֹדוֹ}} (Psalm 72:19).
