(Psalm 90.) The Prayer of Moses the man of God: Lord,
Thou hast been a dwelling for us in every generation. Before
the mountains were born and Thou didst bring forth the
earth and the world, from eternity to eternity Thou art God.
Thou dost restore mortals to contrition and say: Return,
children of men. For a thousand years are in Thine eyes as a
yesterday that passes and as a watch in the night. Thou hast
carried them off: they are in sleep; in the morning he is
renewed like grass. In the morning it blossoms and is re-
newed, in the evening it withers and dries. For we are
consumed through Thine anger and terrified through Thy
wrath. Thou hast placed our sins before Thee, our secret
transgressions before the light of Thy countenance. For all
our days pass away through Thine anger, we end our years
like a fleeting thought. The days of our years — they are
seventy years or, at full strength, eighty years, and their pride
is toil and sorrow, since swiftly severed do we fly away. Who
knows the might of Thine anger and is Thy wrath according to
the fear of Thee? To count our days teach us well so that we
may acquire a wise heart. Turn again, O Lord — how much
longer — and take pity on Thy servants. Satisfy us in the
morning with Thy grace that we may exult and rejoice all our
days. Cause us to rejoice as long as the days when Thou didst
afflict us and the years when we saw misfortune. Let Thy
work become apparent to Thy servants, and Thy splendour
to their children; and may the favour of the Lord our God
be upon us; and firmly establish the work of our hands for
us, the work of our hands, establish it firmly.
