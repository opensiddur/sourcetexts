Translator’s Preface
A new English rendering of our liturgy for the most sacred
days of the year obviously requires a word of introduction.
The Hebrew text on which this present attempt is based,
will be familiar to those who follow the German rite; and
it might well be regarded as a form of perpetuation of the
memory of our blessed martyrs that their own particular
liturgy should have survived intact in far-flung areas of the
English-speaking world.
My aim has been to combine intelligibility with dignity in
contemporary English. Archaisms have been avoided wherever
possible. For instance, the archaic second person singular is
reserved for the Divine and the normal second person plural
of current usage applied to human beings. With regard to
interpretation, the German translation of the late Rabbi
Dr. Selig Bamberger of Hamburg has been closely followed
throughout the numerous passages of mediaeval poetry, which
depend so largely on Scriptural and Aggadic allusions.
Finally, I must acknowledge my gratitude to my husband
and the many friends who have been generous with their
help and advice.
